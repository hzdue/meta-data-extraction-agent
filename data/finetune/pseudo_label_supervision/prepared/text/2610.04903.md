## **Abstract** 

TbMn6Sn6 is a centrosymmetric ferrimagnetic kagome metal that combines topological electronic states with strong magnetic anisotropy and a near-room-temperature spin-reorientation transition (SRT). Real-space imaging studies of thin specimens have revealed helical stripes, biskyrmion-like textures, and skyrmion-bubble-like structures in the spin-reorientation regime. Whether such local textures develop into a bulk long-range-ordered skyrmion lattice remains unresolved. Here we present small-angle neutron scattering (SANS) results probing the mesoscale magnetism of a bulk TbMn6Sn6 single crystal. We observe no sixfold SANS Bragg pattern indicative of a long-range-ordered skyrmion lattice under the explored conditions. Instead, the dominant magnetic response is an anisotropic twofold low- _Q_ scattering component whose intensity distribution and field dependence evolve markedly across the spin-reorientation regime. In zero field, a broad and horizontally elongated low- _Q_ signal develops above _T_ SR and weakens as the system approaches the paramagnetic state. At 345 K with _H ∥ b_ , the signal is diffuse and is suppressed rapidly below about 700 Oe. By contrast, at 310 K with _H ∥ c_ , close to _T_ SR, the response is more concentrated and line-shaped and persists to higher fields. The contrasts between these scans likely reflect the combined effects of rapidly evolving magnetic anisotropy near _T_ SR and the field geometry. The angular intensity distribution for _H ∥ c_ is additionally influenced by the magnetic SANS orientation factor. The intensity-weighted characteristic scattering vector _q[∗]_ and the corresponding _d_ = 2 _π/q[∗]_ are used as descriptive measures of the diffuse reciprocal-space distribution. Our results therefore establish a bulk anisotropic mesoscale magnetic response across the spin-reorientation regime, consistent with stripe- or domain-related textures reported by real-space imaging, while not uniquely identifying their real-space morphology. 

**Keywords:** TbMn6Sn6; kagome magnet; small-angle neutron scattering; spin-reorientation transition; anisotropic magnetic scattering; mesoscale magnetism 

## **1 Introduction** 

Topological magnets provide a platform for connecting real-space spin textures with momentum-space electronic topology. In kagome magnets, this interplay is particularly rich because geometrical frustration, spin-orbit coupling, exchange interactions and magnetic anisotropy can operate on comparable energy scales. TbMn6Sn6 is a prominent member of the _R_ Mn6Sn6 family ( _R_ = rare 

1 

earth). It crystallizes in the centrosymmetric hexagonal _P_ 6 _/mmm_ structure, consisting of Mn kagome layers separated by Tb-Sn layers. The material exhibits ferrimagnetic order below approximately 423 K, and its low-temperature out-of-plane magnetic state is associated with Chern-gapped kagome electronic bands and quantum-limit Chern magnetism [1]. A defining feature of TbMn6Sn6 is that its magnetic anisotropy changes strongly with temperature. The easy magnetization axis switches from the _c_ axis at low temperature to the basal plane through a spin-reorientation process near room temperature, _T_ SR _∼_ 310 K [2, 3, 4]. 

Because the spin-reorientation transition reflects a changing balance among exchange, magnetocrystalline anisotropy, magnetostatic energy and Zeeman energy, it can stabilize mesoscale magnetic textures. Lorentz transmission electron microscopy (L-TEM) has directly observed periodic helical stripe domains in TbMn6Sn6 over a broad temperature range near the spin-reorientation transition and biskyrmion-like textures in a narrower temperature and magnetic-field range [2, 3]. In those works, the helix periodicity was reported to be about 100 nm at 310 K, and the texture evolved to a biskyrmion structure followed by a polarized state under external magnetic field. A subsequent L-TEM study further shows that skyrmion bubbles can be generated near the spin-reorientation domain interface by a convergent electron beam and that the generation is attributed to local anisotropy variations, dipolar effects and domain-wall confinement rather than to a conventional Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction [3]. Magneto-optic Kerr microscopy has also imaged stripy magnetic domains in TbMn6Sn6 and found that the domain contrast degrades with increasing temperature and disappears above the spin-flip temperature [4]. These observations demonstrate that mesoscale domains are strongly coupled to the temperature-dependent magnetic anisotropy of TbMn6Sn6. 

Nevertheless, real-space imaging on thin lamellae and bulk reciprocal-space measurements do not necessarily probe the same magnetic state. Since TbMn6Sn6 is centrosymmetric, bulk DzyaloshinskiiMoriya interactions (DMI) are symmetry-forbidden. The skyrmion-like textures reported in thin specimens are instead associated with a balance among competing exchange interactions, including RKKY-type interactions, dipole–dipole interactions, anisotropy, Zeeman coupling and thicknessdependent magnetostatic energy [2, 3]. Such energy contributions are sensitive to sample thickness, demagnetizing fields, and boundary conditions. A bulk-sensitive method is therefore needed to determine whether the local magnetic textures observed in thin specimens develop into a bulk long-range-ordered skyrmion lattice or instead remain as finite-range stripe-like or domain-wall correlations in a bulk crystal. 

Magnetic SANS is well suited for this problem because it probes bulk magnetic correlations through the Fourier components of the magnetization distribution on mesoscopic length scales, typically from a few to several hundred nanometers [5, 6, 7]. In the small-angle regime, a one-dimensional helix or stripe-like modulation produces a pair of scattering maxima at approximately _±q_ , whereas an ordered hexagonal skyrmion lattice produces sixfold Bragg peaks when the lattice plane is oriented perpendicular to the neutron beam [5, 8]. In more disordered situations, SANS may observe rings, arcs or broad twofold diffuse features rather than sharp spots. Simulations and SANS experiments on disordered skyrmion materials show that labyrinth domains, multidomain helices and rotationally disordered skyrmion states can produce broadened or ring-like reciprocal-space features [9, 10]. Similarly, SANS studies of ferrimagnetic spinels and magnetic multilayers have demonstrated that stripe-like domain-wall patterning can extend throughout a bulk or multilayer sample and can give broad anisotropic small-angle magnetic scattering rather than sharp diffraction peaks [11, 12]. 

Several related systems provide useful context. In centrosymmetric MnNiGa, L-TEM reported biskyrmion nanodomains over a wide temperature range, whereas SANS measurements on bulk single crystals found no evidence for a long-range biskyrmion lattice; nevertheless, the internal twofold axes remained aligned by magnetocrystalline anisotropy [13, 14]. In FeGe thin films, the field response of the helical modulation was found to depend strongly on the field direction [15], while studies of Co8Zn8Mn4 and (Fe,Co)Si showed that disorder, pinning and field history can reorganize mesoscale magnetic textures without producing a simple equilibrium lattice [9, 16]. These examples motivate the present bulk study without prescribing a unique real-space texture. We use SANS to determine how the anisotropic mesoscale magnetic response of TbMn6Sn6 evolves through the spin-reorientation regime and to test whether any long-range sixfold skyrmion-lattice diffraction develops in the bulk.

## **2 Materials and Methods** 

High-quality TbMn6Sn6 single crystals were grown by the self-tin-flux method [1, 2, 3, 4]. The bulk single crystal used for SANS had a mass of 121 mg and dimensions of approximately 6 _._ 7 mm _×_ 5 _._ 7 mm _×_ 0 _._ 6 mm. The crystallographic orientation was checked by Laue diffraction, and the phase purity and orientation were verified by X-ray diffraction. Magnetization measurements were performed with magnetic field applied along representative crystallographic directions in order to locate the spin-reorientation transition and to select suitable temperature and field windows for SANS. 

SANS experiments were performed on the SANS-I instrument of the Swiss Spallation Neutron Source, Paul Scherrer Institute. The incident neutron wavelength was close to 11 Å with a wavelength spread of approximately 10%, and the main sample-detector distance was about 18 m. The neutron beam was aligned along the crystallographic _b_ axis so that the detector plane corresponded approximately to the _ac_ reciprocal-space plane. This geometry and the temperature and magnetic-field ranges were chosen with reference to L-TEM studies that reported helical/stripe and biskyrmion-like textures near the spin-reorientation regime [2, 3]. We measured a zero-field temperature scan across a wider interval than the imaging studies and field scans for both _H ∥ b_ and _H ∥ c_ , thereby characterizing the temperature and field evolution of the low- _Q_ scattering, its anisotropy, and field suppression while also searching for signatures of a bulk long-range-ordered skyrmion lattice beyond the previously reported texture window. For _H ∥ c_ , the angular intensity on the detector also reflects the magnetic SANS orientation factor, because only magnetization components perpendicular to the scattering vector contribute to the magnetic scattering cross section [5, 6]. 

The SANS data were reduced using GRASP [17] and inspected using GRASP-based reciprocalspace visualization procedures [18]. Detector efficiency correction, monitor normalization, masking and transformation to _Qx_ - _Qy_ coordinates were carried out consistently for all scans. The data were intensity-normalized and background-subtracted before further analysis. For each region of anisotropic magnetic scattering, the integrated intensity _I_ used in the main figures was obtained by summing the background-subtracted detector intensity over the corresponding two-dimensional region of interest (ROI). Because the observed low- _Q_ magnetic signal is diffuse rather than resolution-limited Bragg peaks, the characteristic wave vector was extracted from the intensity-weighted first moment of the background-subtracted intensity distribution within each ROI [19, 20, 21]. We first consider the zero-field temperature scan with the beam along _b_ . As shown in Figure 2(a), a broad horizontal twofold low- _Q_ signal emerges near the spin-reorientation temperature, is strongest between roughly 320 and 380 K, and fades on approaching 400 K. This is the same broad temperature interval in which L-TEM and MOKE studies reported helical or stripe-like domain textures [2, 3, 4]. The coincidence in temperature and the twofold anisotropy make stripe- or domain-related correlations a natural real-space interpretation, but the SANS result itself is more generally described as anisotropic mesoscale magnetic scattering. The integrated intensity in Figure 2(b) follows the development and loss of this magnetic contrast. The inverse- _q_ descriptor _d_ in Figure 2(c) varies much less over the same interval. Its numerical values are comparable to the mesoscale spacings reported by real-space imaging [2, 3]; we therefore use it as a secondary descriptor of the diffuse scattering rather than as the defining evidence for a specific stripe periodicity. No sixfold pattern is observed under these conditions.

Figure 3 shows the field scan at 345 K for beam _∥ b_ and _H ∥ b_ . The low- _Q_ response is broad and diffuse. Its integrated intensity falls rapidly up to about 700 Oe and changes only weakly at higher fields; _d_ decreases over the same low-field interval and then approaches a plateau. At 345 K, above _T_ SR and within the high-temperature easy-plane regime, the magnetic anisotropy has evolved toward the high-temperature easy-plane state [22, 23, 24]. An in-plane field can therefore align the magnetization comparatively efficiently, providing a natural explanation for the rapid loss of the anisotropic low- _Q_ contrast. The evolution of _d_ is treated more cautiously. Rather than implying a literal contraction of an ideal stripe period, it is interpreted as being consistent with a redistribution of diffuse spectral weight as the larger-scale low- _q_ component is preferentially suppressed. The crossover field is comparable to the scale on which the _H ∥ b_ magnetization in Figure 1(d) approaches its

Figure 4 presents the _H ∥ c_ scan at 310 K, close to _T_ SR. The magnetic scattering is distinctly more concentrated and line-shaped than that observed at 345 K for _H ∥ b_ , with its dominant intensity distributed approximately perpendicular to the _c_ axis. The signal also decays more gradually with increasing magnetic field and remains visible up to the highest measured field. Because the 310 K and 345 K measurements differ in both temperature and field direction, the contrast observed cannot be uniquely assigned to either variable alone. A physically consistent interpretation is that the rapidly evolving magnetic anisotropy near _T_ SR makes the mesoscale magnetization particularly susceptible to field orientation, while the _H ∥ c_ geometry weights a different set of magnetic Fourier components through the magnetic SANS orientation factor [5, 6, 22, 23, 24]. The observed evolution from a broad horizontal response at 345 K to a concentrated, nearly orthogonal response at 310 K therefore indicates a strongly anisotropic reorganization of the mesoscale magnetic scattering, rather than providing direct evidence for a 90 _[◦]_ rotation of real-space stripes. The field dependence of _d_ is correspondingly used only as a descriptive measure of the evolving diffuse distribution. Its magnitude remains comparable to the mesoscale texture lengths reported in L-TEM studies [2, 3].

and much less systematic in its field dependence than that at 310 K, with the dominant anisotropy returning toward the horizontal direction. This high-temperature behaviour provides an important control, as the line-shaped response observed for _H ∥ c_ at 310 K is not a generic consequence of the scattering geometry, but is more pronounced in the vicinity of the spin-reorientation regime. 

Taken together, the temperature- and field-dependent measurements establish the main result of this work, namely, the bulk low- _Q_ magnetic scattering varies its intensity, angular distribution and field scale across the spin-reorientation regime. At 345 K for _H ∥ b_ , the magnetic scattering response is broad and rapidly suppressed, whereas at 310 K for _H ∥ c_ , the response is more concentrated, nearly orthogonal in reciprocal space and persists to higher fields. This contrast is consistent with the combined effects of the temperature-dependent magnetic anisotropy and field-direction selection. The coincidence with the temperature window of stripe/helical textures seen by real-space imaging supports a stripe- or domain-related microscopic picture, while SANS directly establishes the anisotropic mesoscale scattering rather than a unique real-space texture. No sixfold magnetic diffraction peaks are observed in any measured configuration. 

The absence of sixfold diffraction peaks from a bulk long-range-ordered skyrmion lattice is compatible with the microscopic magnetic ingredients established for TbMn6Sn6. The centrosymmetric _P_ 6 _/mmm_ structure forbids the uniform bulk DMI that provides the conventional route to longwavelength chiral helimagnetism. The absence of conventional bulk DMI removes one common route for fixing chirality and stabilizing a long-range chiral skyrmion lattice, although frustrated exchange and dipolar interactions can support topological textures by other mechanisms. In TbMn6Sn6, nontrivial textures have instead been attributed to a balance among RKKY-type frustrated exchange, dipolar interaction, anisotropy variation, Zeeman energy and the spin-reorientation interface [2, 3]. These ingredients are sensitive to sample thickness, boundary conditions and lamella geometry. The electron-assisted study explicitly showed that homogeneous field and temperature changes do not always produce a bubble lattice, whereas a local SRT interface created by a convergent electron beam can generate skyrmion bubbles [3]. This sensitivity is consistent with the present observation of anisotropic diffuse bulk scattering without a long-range ordered sixfold skyrmion-lattice pattern. More generally, skyrmion theory and experiments show that DMI, frustrated exchange and dipolar energies may all stabilize topological textures under suitable conditions, but positional and orientational order depend on anisotropy, dimensionality, disorder and pinning [25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32]. 

The diffuse nature of the twofold SANS signal indicates that the observed scattering is distributed over a finite region of reciprocal space rather than forming resolution-limited Bragg spots. The quantity _d_ therefore serves as a compact inverse- _q_ descriptor of the radial centroid of the diffuse scattering; its numerical similarity to spacings reported by L-TEM is suggestive, but does not by itself establish a specific real-space texture. The primary evidence in the present work is instead the anisotropic, temperature- and field-dependent magnetic scattering intensity. Broad anisotropic magnetic SANS has been widely observed in domain-patterned and spin-misaligned magnetic systems [5, 6, 11, 12]. This interpretation is consistent with classical stripe-domain and magnetic-domain theory, in which exchange, anisotropy, magnetostatic energy, Zeeman coupling and pinning jointly determine mesoscale domain patterns [33, 34, 35]. Studies of TbMn6Sn6 and related _R_ Mn6Sn6 compounds further reveal competing Mn–Mn and Mn–Tb exchange interactions, rare-earth single-ion anisotropy, and electronic correlations [36, 37, 38, 39]. Neutron diffraction, high-field magnetization and microscopic imaging studies have independently demonstrated the strong sensitivity of the spin-reorientation process to magnetic field and anisotropy [40, 41, 42, 43]. These established energy scales provide a natural framework for understanding the strongly anisotropic and field-dependent diffuse magnetic scattering observed here, without requiring a unique assignment to a particular real-space texture. 

## **4 Conclusions** 

We have carried out a bulk-sensitive SANS study to characterize the mesoscale magnetic response of TbMn6Sn6 across its spin-reorientation regime. The dominant observation is a broad, anisotropic 

8 

twofold low- _Q_ signal rather than a sixfold diffraction pattern from a long-range ordered skyrmion lattice. In zero field, the signal develops near _T_ SR and disappears on approaching the paramagnetic state. At 345 K with _H ∥ b_ , it is diffuse and is suppressed rapidly below about 700 Oe. At 310 K with _H ∥ c_ , it becomes more concentrated and line-shaped and is suppressed more gradually. Because these scans differ in both temperature and field orientation, their contrast is most naturally understood as the combined consequence of the rapidly evolving magnetic anisotropy near _T_ SR and the directional sensitivity of the applied-field geometry, including the magnetic SANS orientation factor. The quantity _d_ is used as a secondary inverse- _q_ descriptor of the diffuse reciprocal-space distribution rather than as a unique real-space stripe period. Its magnitude is comparable to the mesoscale spacings reported by L-TEM and MOKE, while the primary SANS result is the anisotropic magnetic scattering itself. 

These results connect the spin-reorientation transition to a qualitative reorganization of magnetism on mesoscopic length scales. They also separate two levels of information that are often conflated: realspace imaging identifies specific textures in thin specimens, whereas SANS tests how such magnetism is expressed statistically throughout the illuminated bulk volume. Within the explored temperature, field and geometry range, TbMn6Sn6 shows no evidence for a coherent three-dimensional skyrmion lattice, but exhibits a pronounced anisotropic mesoscale response controlled by the spin-reorientation landscape.

Such a subtraction is a standard way to emphasize the field-dependent magnetic contribution when the reference approaches a high-field state, although the finite reference field need not represent perfect saturation [1]. Because the reference subtraction changes the intensity baseline and the relative spectral weight within the ROI, the absolute values of descriptive centroid quantities need not match those obtained with the high-temperature background.
