Strain-tuned (Pr0 _._ 85Y0 _._ 15)0 _._ 7Ca0 _._ 3CoO3 thin films provide a model system for controlling coupled valence, spin-state, structural and metal-insulator transitions (VSSTs) in perovskite cobaltite materials. Here, we use Co _L_ 2 _,_ 3 and Pr _M_ 4 _,_ 5 X-ray Absorption Spectroscopy (XAS), along with Co _L_ 3 edge Resonant Inelastic X-ray Scattering (RIXS), to probe the local electronic structure across the strain-controlled VSST in epitaxial (Pr,Y,Ca)CoO3 thin films. In compressively strained films on YAlO3 (YAO) substrates, cooling through the transition produces Pr[4+] -like spectral weight, suppresses Co[4+] -like character, and favors predominantly low-spin Co[3+] . The changes at the Pr and Co edges are quantitatively consistent with a reduction of approximately 0.15 holes per Co in the Co–O network. By contrast, tensile-strained thin films on La0 _._ 18Sr0 _._ 82Al0 _._ 59Ta0 _._ 41O3 (LSAT) substrates have nearly temperature-independent spectra. Multiplet calculations reproduce the principal XAS lineshapes and local _dd_ excitations at high energy loss. Additionally, low-energy RIXS intensity recovered above the transition is consistent with a combination of Co[4+] -like local excitations and a broader particle–hole continuum associated with the metallic phase. These results provide element-resolved evidence for charge compensation between the Pr and Co–O sublattices and support cooperative coupling among charge-transfer, spin-state, and lattice structure at the strain-controlled VSST. 

## **I. INTRODUCTION** 

The rich variety of spin states and valence states of perovskite cobaltite compounds has been studied for decades, providing a versatile platform for exploring the interplay among orbital, spin, charge, and lattice degrees of freedom. In particular, the close competition among Co[3+] (3 _d_[6] ) spin states in LaCoO3 gives rise to a gradual, thermally driven spin-state crossover from a nonmagnetic low-spin ground state to a state with an increasing population of magnetic excited multiplets [1–3]. Upon substituting La with Pr and Ca, this spin-state transition becomes first-order, and is coupled with a metalinsulator and structural transition [4, 5]. It has also been shown that this transition is fundamentally linked to a valence transition of the Pr ions, from nominally Pr[3+] above the transition to a mixture of Pr[3+] and Pr[4+] below [6–10]. Such valence transitions are possible in the case of Pr ions due to the unusual proximity of the Pr 4 _f_ electrons to the Fermi level relative to other rare-earth ions [11]. In the half-doped Pr0 _._ 5Ca0 _._ 5CoO3 compound, the transition temperature is at roughly 90 K. The bulk

perovskite cobaltite (Pr1 _−y_ Y _y_ )1 _−x_ Ca _x_ CoO3 (PYCCO) has recently attracted considerable interest due to enhancement of the transition temperature of this coupled valence and spin-state transition (VSST) to 135 K, enabled by the Y ions stabilizing the low-temperature, lowvolume phase [10, 12–14]. The VSST occurs simultaneously with a metal-to-insulator (MIT) and a structural transition, suggesting strong correlation between these transitions. Much of the experimental work has focused on the characterisation of bulk samples of similar compounds, primarily involving Pr[3+] , Pr[4+] , Co[3+] high spin (HS, _S_ = 2) and low spin (LS, _S_ = 0), and Co[4+] intermediate spin (IS, _S_ = 3 _/_ 2) states [15]. A recent study on strain-tuned thin film samples of this compound family ( _y_ = 0 _._ 15 _, x_ = 0 _._ 3) has shown that the transition could be stabilized to near room temperature ( _∼_ 245 K) under compressive strain, while tensile strain suppresses this valence transition and induces a long-range ferromagnetic metallic behavior, which is not seen in bulk [11, 16, 17]. Such control of the spin state and valence via strain shows great potential for devices, and motivates a microscopic investigation of how epitaxial strain controls the coupled valence and spin-state transition. In particular, the microscopic origin and causal sequence of the coupled transition remain incompletely understood. 

Theoretical studies have mainly investigated the electronic aspect of the VSST by correlating the valence transition with the structural transition [6, 11]. In particular, Kn´ıˇzek _et al._ performed electronic-structure calculations within the generalized gradient approximation 

2 

with an on-site Hubbard _U_ correction (GGA+ _U_ ), using experimentally determined crystal structures across the transition [6]. These structures exhibit a contraction of the unit-cell volume and changes in the Co–O– Co bond angles, as observed by neutron diffraction [18]. Their calculations showed that part of the occupied Pr 4 _f_ states shifts above the Fermi level upon entering the low-temperature phase, transferring electrons to the hybridized Co(3 _d_ )–O(2 _p_ ) states and giving rise to the valence transition. In this picture, the low-temperature valence transition is associated with a partial oxidation of Pr toward Pr[4+] -like character and a compensating reduction of the Co–O network, thereby depleting Co[4+] -like character across the structural transition and stabilizing the insulating state. This charge-transfer picture is supported by subsequent X-ray absorption and emission spectroscopy studies [7–10]. On warming, this charge transfer is reversed, restoring Co–O hole density and the metallic high-temperature phase. More recent DFT calculations have also shown that epitaxial strain modifies the relative stability of the competing metallic and insulating phases [11]. However, the fundamental cause of the structural and spin-state transition remains incompletely understood, leaving a gap for a complete microscopic mechanism of the VSST and how it is strain-tuned. Co _L_ -edge Resonant Inelastic X-ray Scattering (RIXS) measurements could provide an ion-specific probe of the local spin-state and low-energy excitations that underlie the strain-controlled VSST, which is the motivation for the current work. 

Previous work [11] established strain control of the VSST using thin film epitaxy, temperature dependent X-ray diffraction and electron energy loss spectroscopy, electronic transport, and magnetometry measurements. However, the local Co multiplet excitation spectrum across the strain-enhanced transition has not been directly resolved. This work provides the missing characterization of the local electronic states across the transition with synchrotron spectroscopic techniques by performing X-ray Absorption Spectroscopy (XAS) and RIXS experiments at the Co _L_ edge and Pr _M_ edge of two (Pr0 _._ 85Y0 _._ 15)0 _._ 7Ca0 _._ 3CoO3 samples under tensile and compressive strain on two distinct substrates. We report the ratio between different valence states and spin states across the transition, and use cluster multiplet calculation to extract the nature of low-energy excitations correlated with the transition. Finally, we discuss how the observed spin-state, charge-transfer, and lattice degrees of freedom may cooperate to stabilize the VSST under compressive strain and raise its transition temperature towards room temperature.

## **V. RIXS RESULTS** 

We next use Co _L_ 3-edge RIXS to identify the local excitations associated with the valence/spin-state reconstruction and extract the multiplet parameters across VSST. In Fig. 5, the experimental RIXS energy maps on YAO/PYCCO and LSAT/PYCCO samples at 20 K are presented alongside the population-weighted multiplet calculations based on the XAS-derived Co fractions, excluding the Co[2+] ions in the more bulk-sensitive RIXS. Both films show a broad fluorescence-like continuum, which obscures part of the high-energy multiplet structure, but _dd_ excitations at _∼_ 0.5 eV and _∼_ 0.9 eV remain visible and are reproduced in the multiplet calculations shown in Fig. 5(b,d). Thus, despite this fluorescence background, the reproducible _dd_ features provide constraints on the multiplet parameters. We note that the apparent intensity of the _dd_ excitations near 778.8 eV is enhanced by overlap with the broad fluorescence background, so they appear stronger there than in the simulations. According to the simulated spectra, there should be a strong 1.5 eV _dd_ excitation, which is not resolved experimentally. Its absence in the film spectra likely arises from the strong fluorescence background at this energy and deviations of the strained CoO6 environment from the ideal cubic symmetry assumed in the multiplet calculation, which can split, broaden, and re- 

distribute the calculated _dd_ spectral weight. A similar suppression or redistribution of high-energy _d_ - _d_ spectral weight has been reported in strained LaCoO3 films relative to the bulk [23]. 

A weak Raman-like feature near 80 meV is observed in both the YAO/PYCCO and LSAT/PYCCO spectra that resonates at 780.3 eV [see also Fig. 6]. Its energy lies close to that of the calculated _J_ = 1 _→ J_ = 3 excitation within the Co[3+] HS manifold, making a local multiplet excitation a possible initial assignment. However, the feature is also present in the low-temperature YAO/PYCCO film, for which the XAS decomposition yields no detectable Co[3+] HS contribution and the ground state is nonmagnetic. We therefore assign this feature predominantly to a phonon excitation rather than to the Co[3+] HS multiplet. This interpretation is also consistent with highenergy oxygen phonons near 80 meV [24] reported in related cobaltites. It is most clearly resolved at low temperature because of the narrower elastic and quasielastic response; at higher temperatures, it becomes obscured by the broadened elastic line, preventing a reliable determination of its temperature dependence. 

The most robust RIXS signature of the VSST is instead found at low energy. As shown in Fig. 6, the low-temperature YAO/PYCCO film shows strongly suppressed spectral weight between _∼_ 0.1 and 0.3 eV compared with the tensile LSAT/PYCCO film. This suppression is consistent with the insulating state, in which the available phase space for low-energy electronic excitations is reduced. ## **VI. COOPERATIVE PICTURE OF THE VSST** 

The controlled 10 _Dq_ calculations described above reveal close energetic competition between the Co[3+] LS and HS configurations. A modest change in the local crystal-field splitting is sufficient to switch their energetic ordering, supporting a cooperative feedback mechanism that could account for the strong enhancement of the VSST temperature under compressive heteroepitaxial strain. We start by assuming the system to be below the VSST, where the Co spectral weight consists primarily of Co[3+] LS character, with a smaller Co[4+] -like contribution modeled using the IS configuration. On warming, thermal expansion of the local Co–O environment may reduce the crystal-field splitting and favor increased HS Co[3+] character. The larger effective ionic radius [27] and increased _eg_ occupation of HS Co[3+] could, in turn, 

promote further Co–O bond expansion, providing a positive spin–lattice feedback. This instability may couple to charge transfer between the Pr and Co–O sublattices and to the accompanying metal-insulator transition (see Fig. 8). However, the present data do not establish whether this spin-state redistribution precedes the structural transition or follows it. This provides a possible microscopic route by which the structural instability couples to the Pr-derived states near _EF_ in DFT descriptions of the VSST [6], thereby linking the structural, valence, and metal-insulator transitions [7–10]. Importantly, this mechanism is specific to Pr-based cobaltites due to the unusual proximity of the occupied Pr 4 _f_ states to the Fermi level, whereas the occupied 4 _f_ states of other rareearth ions, such as Nd, lie considerably deeper in energy and do not participate in comparable charge transfer [6]. 

Within this qualitative picture, compressive strain shifts the relative free energies of the competing phases toward the smaller-volume, Pr[3+] /Pr[4+] -mixed, LS Co[3+] - rich insulating state. Warming can then favor the competing expanded phase through the combined effects of thermal expansion, spin-state entropy, increased HS Co[3+] character, and recovery of Co–O hole density. Conversely, tensile strain stabilizes the expanded, HS Co[3+] , Co[4+] -like/Co–O-hole-rich metallic state over the measured temperature range, thereby suppressing the firstorder transition into the collapsed low-spin phase. This picture is necessarily qualitative because the local CoO6 environment does not respond to biaxial strain in epitaxial films as it would to simple hydrostatic pressure; strain is instead accommodated through coupled changes in unit-cell volume, Pr–O bonding, octahedral tilts and distortions, and Co valence/spin state [11]. 

## **VII. CONCLUSION** 

We have performed XAS and RIXS on compressive and tensile strain-tuned thin films of (Pr0 _._ 85Y0 _._ 15)0 _._ 7Ca0 _._ 3CoO3 to estimate the Pr and Co valence populations and Co spin-state populations, and investigate their evolution with temperature. The compressively strained PYCCO film on YAO shows a clear redistribution of Pr valence, Co valence, and Co spin-state spectral weights across the near-roomtemperature VSST at approximately 245 K, whereas the 

7 

tensile-strained PYCCO film on LSAT remains largely temperature independent. The Pr M-edge spectra reveal an effective Pr[4+] -like component below the transition, while Co _L_ 2 _,_ 3-edge XAS and Co _L_ 3 RIXS indicate suppressed Co[4+] and dominant Co[3+] LS weight in the low-temperature insulating phase. The inferred redistribution of Co spectral weight is comparable to that reported for related bulk compounds. The _∼_ 25% Pr[4+] -like component corresponds to roughly 0.15 electrons per Co transferred from the Pr sublattice to the Co–O network, consistent with the corresponding reduction of Co[4+] -like hole character inferred from the Co _L_ 2 _,_ 3-edge analysis. Multiplet calculations reproduce the main Co _L_ 2 _,_ 3-edge XAS trends and identify the low-energy RIXS spectral weights recovered above the transition as associated with Co[4+] excitations and additional continuum-like low-energy electronic excitations in the metallic high-temperature phase. The RIXS response therefore reflects both the increase in the local Co[4+] -like components and the increased low-energy particle–hole excitation phase space accompanying the metal-insulator transition across the VSST. The Pr and Co spectral changes are consistent with charge compensation between the Pr and Co–O sublattices, supporting the interpretation of the VSST in the strain-tuned thin films as a coupled Pr–Co charge-transfer process. The calculations further indicate close competition between Co[3+] LS and HS configurations, providing a natural route for strong spin-state–lattice coupling. 

These results motivate a cooperative picture in which compressive strain stabilizes a collapsed, Pr[4+] -admixed, Co[3+] -rich LS insulating state, while warming restores HS Co[3+] , and increases the population of Co[4+] and a predominant Pr[3+] -like configuration. However, the present measurements do not uniquely determine whether the Co spin-state redistribution precedes or follows the structural transition. Future hysteresis-resolved RIXS/XAS, polarization-dependent RIXS maps and local structural probes of the CoO6 environment will be required to establish the causal sequence of the strain-controlled VSST.

## **Appendix A: TFY XAS data** 

To establish that the temperature-dependent spectral changes discussed in the main text are representative of the film bulk, we measured the Co _L_ 2 _,_ 3-edge XAS spectra in the more bulk-sensitive TFY mode, shown in Fig. 9. The YAO/PYCCO film exhibits a clear redistribution of spectral weights between 50 and 300 K, consistent with the VSST observed in TEY. In contrast, the TFY spectra of the LSAT/PYCCO film remain nearly temperature independent, consistent with the absence of this transition under tensile strain. Moreover, the weak Co[2+] -like feature near 778 eV observed in TEY is absent in TFY, supporting its assignment to a surface CoO contribution. The additional feature near 786 eV in the TFY spectra is attributed to second-order incident photons producing Al _K_ -edge absorption in the substrates and is therefore not intrinsic to the Co _L_ 2 _,_ 3-edge response.
