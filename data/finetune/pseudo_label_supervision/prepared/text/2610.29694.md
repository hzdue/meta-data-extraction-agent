## - Effect of Annealing on Maze Domains in Topological Insulator Magnetic Multilayer Heterostructures

## **Abstract** 

Combining topological insulators with chiral magnetic multilayers offers the opportunity to greatly enhance the spin-orbit torque (SOT) driven manipulation of hosted spin textures, however this also exposes devices to the often poor temperature stability of the commonly used, Bi2X3-based (X is a chalcogenic element), topological insulator films. In this work we demonstrate through _in-situ_ -annealing and polarised neutron reflectometry that the multilayer at a Bi2Se3 interface remains magnetically robust up to a temperature of 350 _[◦]_ C: even as the Bi2Se3 epilayer structurally deteriorates. Through x-ray photoemission electron microscopy, an intermediate annealing temperature of _∼_ 200 _[◦]_ C was found to improve the domain ordering of the multilayer, allowing for field-induced skyrmion nucleation, all whilst sustaining minimal damage to the topological insulator. 

## **1 Introduction** 

Topological insulators (TIs) are a class of materials that offer to substantially reduce the high current densities often required for spin orbit torque (SOT) driven control of magnetism in next-generation spintronic devices. Unlike spin currents generated through the spin Hall effect in heavy metals, spin polarisation is instead generated by current flowing in fully spin-polarised topological surface states (TSS) produced from an inversion of valence and conduction bands in the material bulk [1, 2, 3, 4]. These surface states are robust against backscattering and yield a measurably higher effective spin Hall angle than classic spin Hall materials such as Pt, Ta or _β_ -W [5, 6, 7]. Previous studies have demonstrated both how TIs can measurably exert giant damping-like torques on neighbouring ferromagnets as well as drive magnetisation switching [8, 9, 10, 11, 12]. That TIs have been shown capable of outperforming heavy metals as a SOT source raises the possibility that other spin Hall driven physics should be similarly improved with TI inclusion with most interest being that of spin texture manipulation. 

Magnetic skyrmions, and the magnetic multilayers (MMLs) in which they are known to form, have long been of interest for potential low energy, non-volatile data storage [13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18]. Skyrmions can be nucleated and transported through the application of SOTs from the spin Hall effect [15, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23], raising instead the possibility of TSS-driven skyrmion dynamics. Previous work demonstrated how such topological insulator / magnetic multilayer heterostructures can be constructed with minimal structural or magnetic changes to the overlaying multilayer film [24]. In that case, the commonly used TI Bi2Se3 was used: a van der Waals material known to vertically stack in quintuple layers which causes distinct terracing at the TI surface. These films were shown to host zero field maze domains suitable for hosting topological spin textures when Bi2Se3 film growth was adjusted to minimise such terracing. Subsequent measurement demonstrates a quantitative increase in the damping-like torque exerted on the MML from insertion of a Bi2Se3 epilayer [25].

Bi2Se3 is a material whose above-room temperature properties are already well studied, in large part for its use as a thermoelectric material [26, 27, 28]. Careful consideration and optimisation of film temperature is vital in the thin film growth of Bi2Se3 with a post-growth annealing step often being used [29, 30]. Increasing temperature, however, particularly post-growth, risks damage to the Bi2Se3 and its electrical transport properties through various mechanisms such as crystal decomposition, surface oxidation and Se loss arising beyond _∼_ 200 _[◦]_ C [31, 32]. This may be problematic for TI-MML heterostructures both as annealing is often used to anneal MMLs post-growth – often either to increase the effective perpendicular magnetic anisotropy _K_ eff or the interfacial Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (iDMI) [33, 34, 35, 36, 37] – as well as limiting the temperature range devices can operate due to current-induced Joule heating. TI-metal devices should be expected to be particularly prone to heating due to large parasitic currents that shunt through the low resistance metal films creating increased heat due to the _I_[2] dependence of power dissipation. Knowledge of the heterostructure’s response to heating for optimisation and performance is hence critical. 

Here we demonstrate, by establishing an optimal temperature range, how an annealing step can be implemented to improve the ordering of the magnetic domain structure whilst maintaining the structural integrity of the TI epilayer. In proving the thermal stability of sheet films, we further demonstrate the heterostructures suitability for integration into next-generation spintronic devices. 

## **2 Method** 

In this work we investigate the effect of annealing on a 

Bi2Se3(20)/Ta(1.5)/[Pt(0.8)/CoB(0.6)/Ru(0.5)] _×_ 6/Ru(0.5) stack with perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA), where all given layer thicknesses are in nm. The Bi2Se3 epilayer was grown by molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) on a sapphire substrate before being transferred to an adjoining chamber under UHV to grow the metal films by DC magnetron sputtering. A more detailed description of the sample growth and optimisation can be found in previous work [24]. A schematic of the sample stack can be seen in Fig. 1(a). The sample was annealed _in situ_ on the POLREF beamline at the ISIS neutron and muon source with polarised neutron reflectivity (PNR) data taken after each anneal cycle. Samples were annealed under a rough vacuum where _P ∼_ 10 _[−]_[3] mbar. Here the process pressure remains above the estimated Bi2Se3 vapour pressure up to the furnace temperature limit of 350 _[◦]_ C [47]. The sample was annealed in 50 _[◦]_ C increments for 5 minutes at each temperature set-point up to 350 _[◦]_ C before returning to room temperature for measurement. Measurements were taken with a 0.7 T applied in-plane field to saturate the sample along the magnetic hard axis.

To observe this, polarised neutron reflectometry as a depth resolved magnetic probe was used. From this the spin asymmetry (SA) can be calculated, defined as ( _R_ + _− R−_ ) _/_ ( _R_ + + _R−_ ).

The average domain width, shown in Fig. 4(g), extracted from angular averages of fast Fourier transforms FFTs of the images, was reduced by _∼_ 40% from 600 to 340 nm and becomes more sharply defined with the standard deviation in the fitted log-normal distribution of the FFT peak narrowing by 20% from 1.9 to 1.5 µm _[−]_[1] . This appears to be consistent with the unchanged _H_ k observed after annealing by in-plane SQUID-VSM. 

Also seen at zero field are new skyrmion-like features. These are seen to persist to higher field than the maze domains when an external, out-of-plane field is applied, as shown in Fig. 4(c-f). This relative stability against permutations supports the assumption they are topological. Skyrmions could not similarly be nucleated through magnetic field in the control sample reinforcing the need for prior annealing of the MML. This is similar to studies of Pt/Co-based multilayers showing that thermal treatment aids skyrmion nucleation [41, 42]. It is likely both the improved domain ordering and skyrmion stabilisation are due to an increase in the strength of the interfacial DMI. This would be consistent with the sharpening of the magnetic interfaces seen in PNR slab model fitting [43] and the corresponding increase in _Ki_ from SQUID-VSM magnetometry. 

## **4 Conclusions** 

These samples have proven to be structurally and magnetically robust up to at least 200 _[◦]_ C in agreement with wider literature [8, 12, 29, 30, 31, 32]. This will be a satisfactory range for reliable operation in the context of spintronic devices for most applications. Interdiffusion between the TI and multilayer is effectively suppressed at the interface by the Ta buffer layer given the lack of any magnetic deadness appearing during annealing. Device function under increasing temperature instead appears to be limited by the breakdown in the Bi2Se3 structure driven by Se loss, not the multilayer. The known efficacy of refractory buffer layers as a diffusion barrier against Se [8, 12, 39] is further established and marks the first time this has been demonstrated by PNR. 

The existence of skyrmions in a TI-multilayer heterostructure is promising for future TSS-driven skyrmion motion experiments. Whilst previous studies have shown skyrmions or a corresponding topological Hall effect hosted in other TI-based heterostructures [44, 45], this marks their first documented observation in a heterostructure that combines a traditional ferromagnet - heavy metal multilayer system with a TI. Such multilayer systems are known for being widely customisable through the control of many different growth parameters and may hence prove more versatile in their integration with topologically insulating materials. This is in addition to the physics of SOT-driven spin texture manipulation in chiral multilayers being, at this stage of the

## **S1 Measurement Details** 

## **S1.1 SQUID-VSM** 

Measurements were conducted on a Quantum Design MPMS-3 SQUID-VSM magnetometer and were measured over a _±_ 2 T range at room temperature. These were subsequently corrected for trapped coil flux by field comparison with a Pd reference of known susceptibility and sample shape effects by division of an in-plane or out-of-plane filling factor. 

## **S1.2 PNR** 

Measurements were performed at the POLREF beamline at ISIS Neutron and Muon source. Both spin channels were collected by time of flight detection. The 20 mm _×_ 20 mm sample was attached to a copper puck and thermocouple by thermal paste. Measurements were conducted _in situ_ inside a tube furnace under rough vacuum of _∼_ 10 _[−]_[3] provided by a rotary roughing pump throughout the experiment. The sample would be raised to the desired temperature in increasing 50 _[◦]_ C increments, held at this temperature for 5 minutes before switching off the furnace and allowing the sample to return to room temperature. The 

1 

cooling rate was quickened by running helium gas through the back of the puck. After each anneal step, scattering wave-vectors were collected at three angles: 0.5, 1.2 and 2.3 _[◦]_ for 1, 2 and 8 hours respectively. Data reduction was performed with 10 hours of transmission statistics. 

Slab model fits were modelled using the neutron fitting software Refl1D and fitted using the DREAM algorithm [1]. The model fitted coupled 20 and 350 _[◦]_ C slab models which shared identical nuclear SLD distributions but had independent magnetic models. The combined fit achieves a final chi-squared goodness of fit of _χ_[2] = 6 _._ 787 _±_ 0 _._ 099. The code used is included in the open access repository and builds on that used in the previous study [2]. 

## **S1.3 X-PEEM** 

All XMCD X-PEEM measurements were conducted at the I06 beamline at the Diamond Light Source. Photons were tuned to the Co L3 edge by XAS measurement and images were collected at room temperature and under a 20 kV bias.
