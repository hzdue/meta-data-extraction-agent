Rare-earth substitution strongly modifies the structure, metallicity, and superconducting behavior of Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) nickelates, but its impact on their magnetic interactions remains unclear. Here, we combine x-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) and resonant inelastic x-ray scattering (RIXS) to resolve the multiorbital electronic structure and magnetic excitations of Pr4Ni3O10. The absence of an overlapping rare-earth resonance provides direct access to the Ni _L_ 3 edge, while polarization-dependent measurements at the Ni _L_ - and O _K_ -edges resolve distinct planar and out-ofplane Ni–O hybridization channels. Momentum-resolved RIXS reveals dispersive magnetic excitations that soften toward the incommensurate spin-density-wave wave vector. An effective spin-wave analysis yields a sizable intra-trilayer exchange coupling, comparable to the leading in-plane interactions and robust against rare-earth substitution. Our results identify the multilayer architecture, rather than rare-earth substitution alone, as a key control knob of the magnetic interactions in these materials. 

## **I. INTRODUCTION** 

The emergence of high-temperature superconductivity in bilayer and trilayer Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) nickelates under pressure or epitaxial strain raises questions about how the multilayer Ni–O architecture controls electronic and magnetic interactions[1–5] . Unlike cuprates or square-planar nickelates, these octahedrally coordinated compounds host both active Ni 3 _dx_ 2 _−y_ 2 and 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2 orbitals, with the latter coupling neighboring NiO2 planes through apical oxygen[6,7] . Across the RP series, the number of layers changes the nominal Ni orbital filling and dimensionality of the electronic structure[8] . In bilayer La3Ni2O7, the two NiO2 planes form strongly coupled 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2 bonding and antibonding states, which play an important role in its low-energy electronic and magnetic response[9,10] . Adding a third NiO2 plane produces an additional nonbonding component and creates inequivalent inner and outer Ni sites, which in turn give rise to layer-dependent orbital occupation and magnetic response[11–14] . 

Beyond the number and stacking sequence of the NiO2 planes[2,15–17] , isovalent rare-earth substitution offers a complementary route for tuning the local crystal structure and electronic interactions through chemical pressure[18–20] . This is particularly evident in the comparison between La4Ni3O10 and Pr4Ni3O10, where the smaller Pr ion contracts the lattice and distorts the Ni-O-Ni bond geometry[21–23] . Under pressure, bulk superconductivity in Pr4Ni3O10 reaches an onset temperature near 40 K, surpassing the approximately 30 K onset reported for 

the La compound[3,24,25] . The enhanced superconducting transition temperature of Pr4Ni3O10 is accompanied by a more robust density-wave state, which appears near 160 K at ambient pressure rather than at approximately 140 K in La4Ni3O10 and persists over a broader pressure range[3,24,26] . These contrasting trends underscore the sensitivity of trilayer nickelates to subtle structural changes and raise the question of how rare-earth substitution modifies their underlying electronic and magnetic interactions. 

XAS and RIXS provide direct access to both the multiorbital electronic structure and magnetic interactions of RP nickelates. Ni _L_ - and O _K_ -edge measurements have identified substantial oxygen-ligand contributions and an active manifold of Ni 3 _dx_ 2 _−y_ 2, 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2, and O 2 _p_ states involving both in-plane and out-of-plane Ni– O hybridization[5,27–31] . Momentum-resolved RIXS has further revealed dispersive magnetic excitations and a strong evolution of magnetic interactions with layer number[31–35] . In bilayer La3Ni2O7, the magnetic response is dominated by interlayer coupling through apical oxygens, whereas in trilayer La4Ni3O10 the effective interlayer interaction is reduced to a magnitude comparable to the leading in-plane interactions. Whether this balance survives the structural and electronic changes introduced by Pr substitution remains unclear. Pr4Ni3O10 also offers cleaner access to the trilayer Ni _L_ 3-edge excitations because it avoids the La _M_ 4-Ni _L_ 3 overlap that complicates the interpretation of the Ni response in La-based RP nickelates[5,36] .

Here, we use XAS and RIXS to investigate the electronic and magnetic excitations of Pr4Ni3O10. Polarization-dependent measurements at the Ni _L_ - and O _K_ -edges reveal clear anisotropy between in-plane and out-of-plane Ni–O hybridized states. Momentumresolved Ni _L_ 3-edge RIXS reveals dispersive low-energy magnetic excitations that soften toward the incommensurate SDW wave vector. Spin-wave modeling of their dispersion further indicates a sizable effective coupling between the two outer NiO2 layers, comparable to the leading in-plane interactions. Together, these results reveal the multiorbital electronic response and magnetic interactions of Pr4Ni3O10 and show that the dominant magnetic interactions within the trilayers persist despite the structural and electronic changes introduced by Pr substitution. 

## **II. EXPERIMENTAL METHODS** 

High-quality single crystals of Pr4Ni3O10 were synthesized by high-oxygen-pressure optical floating-zone growth following Ref. 21. At ambient pressure, Pr4Ni3O10 crystallizes in the monoclinic space group _P_ 21 _/a_ . Throughout this work, we use a pseudotetragonal notation with _a_ = _b_ = 3 _._ 829 A and[˚] _c_ = 27 _._ 5[˚] A, and express momentum transfer as **q** = ( _H, K, L_ ) in reciprocal lattice units (r.l.u.). 

We performed XAS and RIXS measurements at the ID32 beamline of the European Synchrotron Ra- 

diation Facility (ESRF) under proposal HC-6368[37] . Polarization-dependent XAS and incident-energydependent RIXS were measured with _σ_ - and _π_ -polarized incident x-rays at _θ_ = 15 _[◦]_ in the ( _H, H, L_ ) scattering plane. Momentum-dependent Ni _L_ 3-edge RIXS was measured with _π_ -polarized incident x-rays in the ( _H, H, L_ ) and ( _H,_ 0 _, L_ ) scattering planes. For the nominal in-plane scans, we fixed the spectrometer arm at 2Θ = 149 _._ 5 _[◦]_ and varied the sample angle to scan _q∥_ = ( _H, H_ ) and ( _H,_ 0), such that the out-of-plane _L_ component varied continuously along each trajectory. To probe the out-ofplane dependence, we varied 2Θ and the sample angle together while fixing _q∥_ = ( _−_ 0 _._ 08 _, −_ 0 _._ 08) r.l.u. and ( _−_ 0 _._ 10 _,_ 0) r.l.u., respectively. The momentum-dependent measurements had an energy resolution of 24.5 meV. All experiments were performed at 30 K, above the _∼_ 26 K transition coupling Pr and Ni magnetic moments[12] , and thus predominantly probe the Ni-sublattice density-wave state. We provide further experimental and analysis details in the Supplemental Material[38] . 

## **III. MULTIORBITAL ELECTRONIC STRUCTURE** 

Pr4Ni3O10 consists of trilayer blocks of corner-sharing NiO6 octahedra separated by Pr–O rock-salt layers [Fig. 1(a)]. Relative to La4Ni3O10, the Pr compound has a more compact but more distorted trilayer structure and considerably lower in-plane metallicity[21,39] . Below the density-wave transition near 160 K, intertwined chargeand spin-density-wave (SDW) order develops, with the charge modulation in phase across the trilayer and the antiphase SDW concentrated on the two outer layers, leaving the inner layer near a magnetic node[11,12,40] . Together with the intrinsic layer differentiation produced by 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2 hybridization, a density-wave-driven redistribution of 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2 occupation may further enhance this nonuniform layer response[13,14,41] . 

Figure 1(b) summarizes the RIXS scattering geometry and orbital channels relevant to the polarizationdependent measurements. In our geometry, the electric field of _σ_ -polarized light lies in the _ab_ plane and predominantly probes in-plane states, whereas _π_ -polarized light has a dominant _c_ -axis component and is more sensitive to out-of-plane states. Microscopically, Ni 3 _dx_ 2 _−y_ 2 states hybridize primarily with planar O 2 _px,y_ orbitals, while Ni 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2 states provide an out-of-plane channel through apical O 2 _pz_ orbitals[5,30,31] . The polarization dependence therefore directly distinguishes the planar and out-of-plane Ni–O hybridized states in Pr4Ni3O10. Figure 1(b) also shows the effective exchange interactions _J_ 1, _J_ 2, _J_ 3, and _Jz_ discussed in the next section, with _Jz_ coupling the two outer layers across the magnetically suppressed inner layer.

Polarization-dependent XAS further reveals the multiorbital character of the unoccupied Ni–O states. At the O _K_ edge [Fig. 2(b)], a pronounced pre-edge appears for both _σ_ and _π_ polarizations, reflecting ligand-hole states formed by strong O 2 _p_ –Ni 3 _d_ hybridization[30] . For _π_ polarization, the pre-edge is shifted lower in energy by approximately 0.2 eV relative to _σ_ polarization. The Ni _L_ 3- edge shows a corresponding energy shift, with two broad spectral components more clearly resolved for _π_ polarization and displaced together by approximately 0.2 eV to lower energy. The similar polarization-dependent shifts at both the Ni _L_ 3 and O _K_ edges indicate a common anisotropy of the hybridized Ni–O states, while the slightly broader _π_ -polarized profiles are consistent with interlayer-hybridized molecular orbitals involving innerapical O 2 _pz_ hopping[13,31] . Extended XAS measurements for both Ni _L_ 2 _,_ 3-edges and a broader O _K_ -edge energy range are reported in Supplemental Material Sec. S3. 

We next examine the incident-energy dependence of the RIXS response across the Ni _L_ 3 and O _K_ reso- 

nances in Fig. 3. Consistent with the XAS results, we find that the resonant incident energy shifts to lower energy for _π_ polarization. At both edges, the RIXS maps resolve characteristic electronic excitations of RP nickelates, including Raman-like _dd_ excitations centered near 1 eV, broader charge-transfer features above approximately 2 eV, and fluorescence-like spectral weight extending along the diagonal direction[5,31,33] . The relative spectral weights differ between the two edges, as the Ni _L_ 3 resonance [Figs. 3(a) and (b)] features local _dd_ excitations, whereas the O _K_ resonance [Figs. 3(d) and (e)] is more sensitive to the charge-transfer response[29–31] . We observe little polarization dependence in the fluorescencelike continuum, consistent with its origin in more extended hybridized Ni–O states with comparable contributions from the 3 _dx_ 2 _−y_ 2 and 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2 orbitals[31] . By contrast, the incident-energy-integrated Ni _L_ 3-edge spectra in Fig. 3(c) show a clear enhancement of the _dd_ manifold for the _π_ polarization, indicating a larger contribution from Ni 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2-derived states to the orbital-excitation response. We also find that the _dd_ excitation envelope in Pr4Ni3O10 is relatively broad. This broadening may arise from a shorter lifetime of the _dd_ excitations, although contributions from the more distorted and inequivalent NiO6 environments within the trilayer may also be present. Comparison with La4Ni3O10 in Supplemental Material Sec. S5 confirms the broader orbitalexcitation response of the Pr compound and the absence of the broad 3–6 eV La-hybridized charge-transfer response. 

At the O _K_ edge [Figs. 3(d) and (e)], we find the strongest polarization dependence in the charge-transfer sector. The integrated spectra in Fig. 3(f) remain similar below approximately 3 eV but diverge at higher energy loss. The _σ_ -polarized response forms a sharper feature near 6 eV, whereas the _π_ -polarized response extends over a broader energy range. These distinct line shapes indicate different distributions of charge-transfer final states for the predominantly planar and out-of-plane oxygen channels. We associate the broader _π_ -polarized response with strong interlayer hybridization between inner-apical O 2 _pz_ and Ni 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2 states, which splits the trilayer 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2-derived states into bonding, nonbonding, and antibonding molecular subbands and distributes the corresponding O 2 _pz_ spectral weight over a wider energy range[13,31] . The sharper _σ_ -polarized feature instead reflects planar O 2 _px,y_ –Ni 3 _dx_ 2 _−y_ 2 response distributed over a narrower energy range[31] . At much lower energy loss, not resolved on the scale of the maps shown above, we also observe a pronounced excitation near 50 meV, consistent with phonons previously detected by O _K_ -edge RIXS in RP nickelates[30,31] . This mode is resonantly enhanced near the O _K_ pre-edge and is most clearly visible for _σ_ polarization. Enlarged low-energy maps and further discussion are provided in Supplemental Material Sec. S6.

## **IV. MAGNETIC EXCITATIONS AND INTERLAYER COUPLING** 

We now examine the momentum dependence of the low-energy Ni _L_ 3-edge RIXS response [Figs. 4(a)–(d)]. Using _π_ -polarized incident x-rays tuned to the first Ni _L_ 3 resonance near 855.8 eV, we resolve dispersive lowenergy excitations along both nominal in-plane directions ( _H, H_ ) and ( _H,_ 0), with fitted energies spanning approximately 20–85 meV. Their magnetic assignment is supported by polarimetric RIXS measurements on related RP nickelates[5,34,35] . The most pronounced evolution occurs along ( _H, H_ ), where the excitation softens as _H_ approaches _−_ 0 _._ 31 r.l.u. and spectral weight accumulates near zero energy loss. This momentum coincides with the incommensurate SDW wave vector established by diffraction, directly connecting the dispersive excitation to the 

density-wave magnetic state[11,12] . The spectra also contain a broad mid-infrared (MIR) continuum extending to several hundred meV before merging into the _dd_ excitations. The MIR response resonates at the Ni _L_ 3 edge and varies only weakly with momentum, consistent with a resonantly enhanced metallic Ni–O background. Its weaker intensity in Pr4Ni3O10 relative to La4Ni3O10, consistent with the lower metallic conductivity of the Pr compound, makes the low-energy magnetic excitations more clearly resolved[5,34,35] . A direct comparison is provided in Supplemental Material Sec. S5. 

At most momenta, we resolve one or two magnetic excitations. Where the response is weaker or approaches the elastic line, we detect a broad low-energy feature and cannot separate individual modes. We extract the excitation energies by fitting the spectra with an elastic line, a smooth MIR contribution, and damped magneticexcitation components. For clarity, Fig. 4 shows the intensity maps after subtracting the fitted elastic contribution; complete spectral decompositions and fitting details are provided in Supplemental Material Sec. S9. The _L_ scans reveal a pronounced modulation of the magnetic spectral weight, while the fitted excitation energies vary more modestly. Models based on magnetic diffraction suggest a configuration with antiparallel outer-layer moments and the inner layer near a node of the SDW modulation[11,12] . The observed periodicity of the magnetic excitations here is consistent with this ground state spin configuration. By contrast, introducing a sizable inner-layer moment would instead produce an up–down– up configuration and double the spectral-weight modulation periodicity along _L_ , inconsistent with the data[31] .

To quantify the interactions underlying the measured dispersion, we model the response using linear spin-wave theory for an effective outer-layer SDW model[5,31,34,35] , implemented using the Sunny package[42] . The in-plane interactions _J_ 1, _J_ 2, and _J_ 3 couple first-, second-, and third-nearest-neighbor sites within the two outer NiO2 layers, whose ordered moments are opposite in phase. We therefore use _Jz_ to parameterize the effective coupling between the two outer-layer SDW components across the trilayer. Pr4Ni3O10 also contains two orthogonal structural-twin domains that contribute to spatially averaged x-ray measurements[43] . Following Ref.[31] , we therefore calculate the nominal ( _H, H_ ) response as an equalweight incoherent average of the two twins, with SDW propagation vectors along ( _H, H_ ) and ( _H, −H_ ). 

A simultaneous fit to the two nominal in-plane scans and two fixed- _q∥ L_ scans gives _J_ 1 = 15 _._ 7(3) meV, _J_ 2 = 1 _._ 7(3) meV, _J_ 3 = 9 _._ 8(1) meV, and _Jz_ = 15 _._ 8(6) meV. The in-plane exchange couplings are constrained in the fitting to correctly capture the incommensurate SDW wavevector (Supplemental Material Sec. S10[38] ). The calculated response [Figs. 4(e)–(h)] reproduces both the magnetic-excitation dispersion and the relative intensity 

distribution along both in-plane and out-of-plane trajectories. Most notably, the effective coupling _Jz_ is comparable to, and slightly larger than, the nearest-neighbor in-plane coupling _J_ 1, despite spanning the full trilayer across the middle NiO2 layer. This unusually large out-of-plane interaction is consistent with the strong Ni 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2–O 2 _pz_ hybridization resolved via XAS, suggesting that the substantial participation of this out-of-plane orbital channel supports magnetic coupling across the trilayer. The importance of this pathway is reinforced by comparison with square-planar R4Ni3O8, where the absence of apical oxygen suppresses out-of-plane hybridization and the dominant magnetic exchange remains within the NiO2 planes, supporting an important role for apical-oxygen-mediated hybridization in the sizable outof-plane magnetic interaction of Pr4Ni3O10[28] . 

The comparable in-plane and out-of-plane interaction scales place the trilayer near an intermediate-coupling regime where conventional spin-wave theory is not fully controlled[44,45] . We therefore further test the robustness of the extracted interlayer coupling using complementary entangled-dimer calculations, together with a stripe-dimer calculation described in Supplemental Ma- 

6 

terial Sec. S11. These alternative descriptions yield _Jz ≃_ 15–21 meV, comparable to the leading in-plane interactions and consistent with the linear-spin-wave result. In this intermediate-coupling regime, neither an expansion about ordered moments, as in conventional spin-wave theory, nor a strong-dimer expansion is fully controlled; nevertheless, both consistently require a substantial effective interlayer coupling. Recent RIXS studies of La4Ni3O10 find similar interaction scales, with leading in-plane interactions of _∼_ 10–16 meV and effective interlayer couplings of _∼_ 20–22 meV[31,34,35] . The interlayer interaction is therefore similar between Prand La-based trilayer compounds, in sharp contrast to bilayer La3Ni2O7, where it reaches approximately 50– 70 meV and substantially exceeds the in-plane interactions[5,31,46,47] . 

More broadly, although the local-moment descriptions above provide good descriptions of the observed dispersions, the metallic nature of these materials suggests that charge degrees of freedom may also play a role in the magnetism in trilayer nickelates. The fitted exchange parameters therefore represent effective interaction scales incorporating the combined effect of local moments together with electronic correlations and itinerant band effects. Recent multiband Hartree-Fock and random-phase approximation (RPA) calculations reproduce the outerlayer-dominated SDW structure and spin-wave-like collective excitations within an itinerant framework, without assuming preexisting localized moments[48–50] . However, extending such self-consistent calculations to the experimentally observed incommensurate state of trilayer nickelates such as Pr4Ni3O10, including its charge modulation and full trilayer orbital structure, would require a substantially larger magnetic unit cell than the commensurate approximations used so far. 

## **V. CONCLUSIONS** 

In summary, we probed the multiorbital electronic structure and magnetic excitation spectrum of Pr4Ni3O10 using XAS and RIXS. Polarization-dependent measurements distinguish planar and out-of-plane Ni–O hybridization channels, while momentum-resolved RIXS constrains the magnetic interactions within the trilayer and reveals the influence of the incommensurate SDW state on the collective magnetic response. ## **S2. XAS AND RIXS MEASUREMENTS** 

X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) and high-energy-resolution resonant inelastic x-ray scattering (RIXS) measurements were performed at the ID32 beamline of the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility (ESRF) [2]. After mounting the sample on the four-circle goniometer, its crystallographic orientation was refined _in situ_ using the (002) and (013) Bragg reflections, measured with the fundamental soft-x-ray beam and its second harmonic, respectively. All XAS and RIXS measurements were performed at 30 K, well below the SDW transition temperature, but above the _∼_ 26 K transition coupling Pr and Ni magnetic moments [3]. 

XAS spectra at the Ni _L_ 2 _,_ 3 and O _K_ edges were collected simultaneously in total-electron-yield (TEY) and totalfluorescence-yield (TFY) modes. The TFY signal was measured with a Si photodiode positioned at a scattering angle of 2Θ = 160 _[◦]_ . Polarization-dependent XAS measurements were performed with both _σ_ - and _π_ -polarized incident x- rays ( _θ_ = 15 _[◦]_ ), providing sensitivity predominantly to in- and out-of-plane unoccupied electronic states, respectively. 

We measured XAS spectra by following two incident-energy acquisition protocols. High-resolution spectra over selected energy windows were acquired using point-by-point scans, with the monochromator stabilized at each prescribed incident energy before recording the detector signals. These scans provided the more reliable relative incident-energy calibration and were used to resolve the Ni _L_ 3 and _L_ 2 absorption edges and the O _K_ -edge pre-edge region. Complementary continuous scans were acquired while sweeping the monochromator through the selected energy range without stopping at individual energy points. Their substantially shorter acquisition time enabled measurements over broader energy windows, encompassing the full Ni _L_ 2 _,_ 3-edge region for comparison of the relative spectral weights of the two edges and an extended range across the O _K_ edge. 

For the RIXS measurements, the spectrometer arm was fixed at a scattering angle of 2Θ = 149 _._ 5 _[◦]_ , except for

the out-of-plane momentum-dependent scans. Incident-energy-dependent RIXS measurements across the Ni _L_ 3 edge (855 _._ 3–858 _._ 3 eV) and O _K_ edge (528 _._ 85–530 _._ 85 eV) were performed with both _σ_ - and _π_ -polarized incident x-rays in the ( _H, H, L_ ) scattering plane at an incidence angle of _θ_ = 15 _[◦]_ . In-plane and out-of-plane momentum-dependent spectra were collected with _π_ -polarized incident photons at the magnon resonance energy of approximately 855 _._ 8 eV. 

The overall RIXS energy resolution was determined from the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the elasticscattering profile measured from amorphous carbon. Momentum-dependent Ni _L_ 3-edge measurements were performed with a narrower monochromator exit slit, yielding an overall energy resolution of 24.5 meV. For the incident-energydependent measurements, a wider exit slit was used to increase the incident photon flux and reduce acquisition times. The resulting broader incident-photon bandwidth yielded overall energy resolutions of 26 meV at the Ni _L_ 3 edge and 34 meV at the O _K_ edge. ## **S3. POLARIZATION-DEPENDENT XAS OF** Pr4Ni3O10 

We measured polarization-dependent XAS at the Ni _L_ 2 _,_ 3 and O _K_ edges using both TEY and TFY detection. In TEY, we recorded the sample drain current generated by emitted electrons, making the signal predominantly sensitive to the near-surface region because of their short escape depth. In TFY, we detected the emitted x-ray fluorescence and therefore probed substantially deeper into the bulk, although saturation and self-absorption can modify relative peak intensities. Because TEY is more surface sensitive, it is also more susceptible to contamination, degradation, and reconstruction. As shown in Figs. S2 and S3, we observe consistent absorption features and polarization dependence in both detection channels, indicating that the near-surface electronic structure is broadly representative of the bulk and showing no evidence of pronounced surface degradation or of a distinct surface phase. 

We measured both _σ_ and _π_ incident polarizations under grazing-incidence conditions. The electric field of the _σ_ -polarized beam lies within the crystallographic _ab_ plane, whereas the _π_ -polarized beam has a dominant _c_ -axis component, providing sensitivity to in- and out-of-plane unoccupied electronic states, respectively. To compare the two polarizations, we normalized each spectrum using the intensities at the low- and high-energy endpoints of the measured range, setting the former to zero and the latter to unity: _I_ norm( _E_ ) = _I_ ( _EI_ (right _E_ ) _−_ ) _I−_ ( _EI_ (l _E_ eftleft) )[.][This][normalization] assumes negligible resonant absorption sufficiently below the edge and a polarization-independent absorption level sufficiently above it. It removes differences in overall offset and scale while preserving the polarization-dependent line shape within each energy window. 

Figure S2 shows point-by-point incident-energy scans near the Ni _L_ 3-, Ni _L_ 2-, and O _K_ -edges, with TEY and TFY spectra in the upper and lower rows, respectively. We observe a clear polarization dependence at the Ni _L_ 3- edge and in the O _K_ - pre-edge region, whereas the Ni _L_ 2 response is less anisotropic. The Ni _L_ 3 spectra contain a lower-energy main resonance and a broader higher-energy feature with stronger ligand-hole and Ni–O-hybridized character. Both features are shifted to lower incident energy for _π_ polarization by approximately 0.2 eV. The higherenergy component is also broader for _σ_ polarization, indicating a polarization-dependent distribution of the hybridized unoccupied states. We observe a comparable shift of approximately 0.2 eV at the O _K_ pre-edge. Together, the Ni _L_ 3- and O _K_ -edge spectra reveal a clear energetic and line-shape anisotropy between the predominantly in-plane and out-of-plane components of the hybridized Ni–O electronic structure. We complemented the point-by-point measurements with continuous incident-energy scans over broader energy ranges, shown in Fig. S3. At the Ni _L_ edge, these scans cover the full _L_ 2 _,_ 3 region and allow direct comparison of the relative edge intensities and their polarization dependence. At the O _K_ edge, they reveal higher-energy absorption features beyond the pre-edge region resolved in the point-by-point scans. The continuous scans thus provide an overview of the full Ni _L_ 2 _,_ 3- and O _K_ -edge responses, while the point-by-point measurements provide more accurately calibrated spectra near the absorption edges.

## **S10. LINEAR-SPIN-WAVE MODELING OF THE MAGNETIC EXCITATIONS** 

To describe the momentum-dependent magnetic response, we used linear spin-wave theory (LSWT) for an effective Heisenberg model of the spin-density-wave state. Although Pr4Ni3O10 is metallic and its magnetic order may have a substantial itinerant component, this model provides a minimal phenomenological description of the collective excitation spectrum. Similar Heisenberg descriptions have been used for magnetic excitations in both bilayer and trilayer Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates [6, 12–14]. 

## **A. Effective exchange model** 

The magnetic unit comprises the two outer NiO2 layers of a trilayer block. We assign no ordered moment to the middle NiO2 layer, consistent with diffraction measurements placing it near a node of the SDW modulation [3]. The two outer layers carry oppositely phased ordered moments coupled by an effective interlayer exchange interaction. The Hamiltonian is

where _ℓ_ = 1 _,_ 2 labels the two outer NiO2 layers. Within each layer, _J_ 1, _J_ 2, and _J_ 3 couple first-, second-, and third-nearest-neighbor Ni sites. The parameter _Jz_ couples vertically aligned sites in the two outer layers. Positive values denote antiferromagnetic exchange. The effective outer-layer coupling _Jz_ should not be interpreted as a direct exchange through the nominally nonmagnetic middle Ni site. Instead, it represents the net interaction between the magnetic outer-layer degrees of freedom after integrating out the middle-layer electronic states. A sizable _Jz_ is physically plausible because the partially occupied Ni 3 _d_ 3 _z_ 2 _−r_ 2 orbitals hybridize strongly along the _c_ axis through apical O 2 _pz_ states. Hence, the resulting interlayer hopping can generate an appreciable magnetic interaction even when the static ordered moment of the middle layer is strongly suppressed. This mechanism is closely related to the strong interlayer coupling discussed in other Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates [12, 14]. 

We use an effective spin length _S_ = 1, so the reported exchange parameters correspond to the energy scales _SJi_ . Because Pr4Ni3O10 is metallic, these parameters provide a phenomenological description of the collective SDW excitations rather than purely localized-spin superexchange constants. ## **S11. ENTANGLED-DIMER SPIN-WAVE CALCULATIONS** 

Conventional LSWT treats the two outer-layer moments as separate semiclassical spins and expands about their classically ordered state. The interlayer exchange _Jz_ couples the moments and their spin-wave fluctuations, but the reference state does not include local entanglement within an outer-layer pair. To assess the possible role of such correlations, we also considered an entangled-dimer formulation in which the two outer-layer spins within each trilayer are treated as a single quantum unit [16, 17]. For the 3 _×_ 3 and 13 _×_ 13 calculations below, the dimer model retains the same exchange network as conventional LSWT, with in-plane couplings _J_ 1, _J_ 2, and _J_ 3 and an effective outerlayer coupling _Jz_ . ## **A. Commensurate supercell approximation** 

We performed the entangled-dimer calculations using finite _N × N_ real-space supercells. Periodic boundary conditions require _|ψ_ **R** + _N_ **a** _⟩_ = _|ψ_ **R** _⟩_ , and likewise along the second in-plane lattice direction, restricting the allowed propagation vectors to ( _h, k_ ) = ( _m/N, n/N_ ) with integers _m_ and _n_ . An arbitrary incommensurate wave vector therefore cannot be represented exactly in a finite periodic supercell. This restriction arises from the present supercell implementation rather than from the dimer description itself. Although a generalized entangled spiral could in principle describe an incommensurate state, the `EntangledSystem` calculation represents the spatial modulation explicitly within the real-space supercell. The experimental SDW vector _δ ≃_ 0 _._ 31 must therefore be approximated by a nearby rational fraction. Near the ordering vector imposed by the supercell, the calculated spectrum contains very low-energy modes and the linear spin-wave calculation becomes numerically ill-conditioned. At some nearby momenta, no stable physical spectrum is obtained. We omit these points from the maps and the resulting blank regions therefore reflect numerical limitations of the commensurate supercell calculation.

## **E. Comparison with the conventional LSWT description** 

The different spin-wave descriptions give a consistent picture of the magnetic interactions in Pr4Ni3O10. The conventional LSWT fit gives _Jz_ = 15 _._ 8 meV, the 3 _×_ 3 entangled-dimer fit _Jz_ = 14 _._ 8 meV, and the 13 _×_ 13 calculation shows the best qualitative agreement with the measured _L_ dependence for _Jz ≃_ 15 meV. The stripe-dimer model yields a somewhat larger value, _Jz_ = 21 _._ 4 meV. Despite differences in magnetic structure and treatment of the outerlayer spins, all models require a substantial interlayer interaction on the same energy scale as the dominant in-plane exchanges. This interaction hierarchy differs from the strongly dimerized limit reported for bilayer La3Ni2O7, where the interlayer coupling dominates the magnetic response. In Pr4Ni3O10, the results instead support a more balanced network of significant in-plane and interlayer interactions. The agreement between the conventional and entangleddimer calculations further shows that the inferred interlayer energy scale is not strongly modified by explicitly including local quantum entanglement between the two outer-layer moments.
