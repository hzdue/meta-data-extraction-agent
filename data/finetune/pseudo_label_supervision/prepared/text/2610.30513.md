Predicting speciation in concentrated electrolytes requires accurate ion interactions and sufficient sampling of slowly interconverting coordination environments. We combine Zn K-edge extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) measurements with long-timescale molecular dynamics (MD) driven by machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) to investigate aqueous ZnCl2 from dilute to 30 m. Using active learning, we train revPBE-based DeepMD potentials with D3 corrections included or excluded for Zn-containing pairs and compare them with DPMD-SCAN, classical MD (CMD), and MACE-MATPES-r2SCAN. We benchmark related EXAFS calculations against hydrated and chloride-coordinated Zn reference complexes, calculate spectra from MD ensembles, and examine Zn–Cl coordination free energies. Increasing concentration reduces octahedral Zn coordination, promotes replacement of water by chloride, and increases chloride-bridged association. Although all three DeepMD models yield similar EXAFS at 3 m, DPMD-SCAN shows greater transferability across concentrations, reproducing measured spectral trends, capturing key features of mixed ZnCl2/LiCl spectra, and predicting octahedral fractions consistent with other studies. DFT treatment strongly affects coordination free energies and aggregation, while excluding Zn-related D3 corrections does not systematically improve EXAFS agreement. CMD shows larger spectral discrepancies and less consistent coordination trends. MACE-MATPES-r2SCAN improves upon CMD and produces spectra comparable to DPMD-SCAN, but DPMD-SCAN provides more consistent agreement and greater computational efficiency. Neighbor-resolved analysis reveals that distance disorder and interference among scattering paths can produce similar EXAFS signatures for distinct coordination ensembles. This highlights the importance of rigorous molecular sampling and ensemble-averaged spectral calculations to complement EXAFS when interpreting isolated versus chloride-bridged Zn speciation. 

## **I. INTRODUCTION** 

Electrolyte speciation, defined as the distribution of ions among distinct coordination and association environments, plays a key role in determining the structural, thermodynamic, and transport properties of concentrated aqueous solutions.[1–9] Predicting these populations across a broad concentration range requires an accurate description of competing ion–water and ion–ion interactions,[10–19] which govern the relative free energies of different coordination states,[20–22] together with sufficient configurational sampling to obtain converged speciation statistics. These challenges are particularly pronounced for multivalent ions, where small errors in the description of these interactions can substantially alter the predicted properties.[23–29] 

Aqueous ZnCl2 provides a representative system for investigating these challenges. At low chloride activity, Zn[2+] is predominantly hydrated in an approximately octahedral environment.[30–33] As concentration and chloride activity increase, chloride progressively replaces water in the first coordination shell, producing a distribution of [ZnCln(H2O)m][2–n] coordination environments.

At higher concentrations, these environments can become connected through shared chloride ligands, forming bridged and multinuclear Zn–Cl structures.[8] Consequently, the number of chloride neighbors around a Zn center does not necessarily identify a distinct monomeric species. Distinguishing local coordination from molecular connectivity is therefore important for understanding speciation in concentrated electrolytes. 

Extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) spectroscopy provides element-specific information about the local coordination environment of ions such as Zn[2+] , including the identities of neighboring atoms, interatomic distances, average coordination numbers, and structural disorder.[34–38] However, extracting a unique speciation distribution from EXAFS is challenging because the measured spectrum represents an average over different coordination environments. Coordination numbers and Debye–Waller factors are correlated in conventional EXAFS fitting, and the relatively close ion–O and ion–Cl first-shell distances complicate their separation in real space.[31,32] Furthermore, isolated complexes and chloridebridged multinuclear structures may have similar firstshell environments and therefore produce similar EXAFS signals. Thus, EXAFS provides important experimental constraints on local coordination but generally requires additional structural information to determine detailed molecular speciation. 

Molecular simulations provide complementary infor-

mation by generating structural ensembles from which coordination populations, molecular connectivity, and theoretical EXAFS spectra can be obtained.[37,39] Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) describes ion association and ligand exchange without imposing fixed bonding topologies, but its computational cost limits the accessible timescales and system sizes.[40,41] Although AIMD is well suited to studying the hydration of individual ions, determining speciation in concentrated solutions requires extensive sampling of collective ionic configurations to obtain statistically converged populations. Classical molecular dynamics (CMD) enables substantially longer simulations, but conventional fixed-charge force fields may not accurately capture the competing metal–water, metal–chloride, and solvent-mediated interactions.[42,43] Such inaccuracies can affect both the relative free energies of coordination states and the barriers separating them, potentially overestimating ligand-exchange barriers relative to first-principles predictions.[13,44,45] Consequently, even long CMD trajectories may yield biased or poorly converged speciation populations. 

Machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) offer a promising route to bridge the gap between AIMD and conventional classical force fields by approximating electronic-structure potential-energy surfaces at a fraction of the computational cost.[46–57] This approach enables simulations of larger electrolyte systems over longer timescales while retaining a first-principles-based description of the competing interactions that govern coordination equilibria. For electrolyte solutions, an important question is whether the trained MLIP remains predictive across concentrations as local coordination and ion association environments change. Here, transferability across concentrations refers to this ability to describe different concentration regimes without retraining at each composition, rather than transferability to unrelated chemical systems.[50,52,57] Such transferability is not guaranteed, because changes in concentration can introduce local environments that are inadequately represented in the training data.[22,58] 

Recent work on aqueous solutions demonstrated that the choice of training concentration can strongly influence transferability across concentrations,[22,58] while studies of ionic-liquid mixtures have demonstrated related transferability across mixture compositions.[59] In this regard, recent DeepMD simulations of aqueous ZnCl2 have extended to tens or hundreds of nanoseconds across a broad concentration range, revealing concentration-dependent changes in local coordination and ion association.[8] Nevertheless, long trajectories alone do not guarantee converged speciation populations, particularly when transitions between coordination states are infrequent. Simulations starting from independent initial configurations can help assess convergence, while targeted enhanced sampling, such as umbrella sampling along Zn–Cl separation and coordination number, can provide complementary information about association free energies and barriers. 

The reliability of MLIP-based speciation predictions also depends on the underlying DFT description. Even when an MLIP accurately reproduces reference DFT energies and forces, errors in the exchange–correlation functional or dispersion treatment can alter the relative stability of competing coordination environments. Kostal _et al._ demonstrated that conventional D3 dispersion corrections can distort the hydration structures of alkali and alkaline-earth cations because their dispersion coefficients are based on neutral-atom reference states.[60] Although Zn[2+] was not examined, these findings motivate assessing the influence of dispersion corrections on Zn coordination environments. These considerations highlight the importance of evaluating coordination free energies, the convergence of speciation populations, and agreement with experimental structural measurements, rather than relying solely on the accuracy of reference energies and forces. 

Recent advances in computational spectroscopy have strengthened the connection between simulated structures and X-ray absorption measurements.[61,62] For aqueous ZnCl2, Cao _et al._ combined experimental Zn K- edge X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) spectroscopy with an interpretable graph-neural-network model trained on first-principles spectra to relate local coordination environments to near-edge spectral features.[63] A complementary challenge is to determine how sensitively experimental extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) spectra constrain the structural ensembles and coordination populations predicted by different interaction models. Because EXAFS reflects an ensemble average over coexisting local environments, independent structural analyses are essential for assessing the extent to which spectral agreement constrains the underlying speciation. 

In this work, we combine active learning,[64,65] longtimescale molecular dynamics, targeted enhanced sampling, and experimental EXAFS spectroscopy to investigate the concentration-dependent structure and speciation of aqueous ZnCl2. We examine how the choice of DFT functional and dispersion treatment influences the predictions of DeepMD-based potentials and compare their accuracy and performance with a universal MLIP and conventional classical MD. Independent simulations and coordination free-energy calculations provide complementary assessments of sampling convergence and the stability of competing coordination environments. We focus on comparing ensemble-averaged EXAFS spectra calculated from MD configurations with experimental measurements across a broad concentration range, including highly concentrated solutions where structural interpretation becomes increasingly challenging. Concentrationdependent changes are further examined using radial distribution functions, coordination numbers, and local coordination geometry. Section II describes the computational and experimental methods, Sec. III presents the results and discussion, and Sec. IV summarizes the main conclusions.

## **II. NUMERICAL & EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS** 

## **A. Custom-trained ML Training Methodology** 

We employed an iterative active-learning strategy using the ArcaNN software package[64] to explore the complex free-energy landscape of aqueous ZnCl2 solutions and train the machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP). The initial training configurations were obtained from enhanced-sampling _ab initio_ molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations of aqueous ZnCl2 performed in this work. The Zn–Cl separation and the chloride coordination number around Zn[2+] were used as collective variables, as described in Section II D. We also used configurations from a previously reported neural-networkpotential study of aqueous ZnCl2,[8] which provided a broad set of equilibrium solution structures across various concentrations. These initial configurations served only as the seed dataset. The subsequent active-learning cycles systematically expanded the training set by identifying and labeling poorly represented regions of configuration space, including subtle changes in local coordination and charge-state environments, as described below. The compositions of the aqueous ZnCl2 systems included in the training dataset are summarized in Table I. 

For labeling all configurations, single-point calculations of the atomic forces and total energies were performed using the CP2K QuickStep module[66] within density functional theory (DFT).[67,68] Various exchange-correlation functionals and dispersion treatments were considered. The functionals included revPBE[69] . Motivated by the recent work of Jungwirth _et al._ ,[60,70] D3 dispersion contributions[71] involving the Zn[2+] cation were excluded from the main revPBE calculations. Valence electrons were treated explicitly, while the core electrons of all atoms were represented by norm-conserving Goedecker–Teter–Hutter (GTH) pseudopotentials.[72] Wavefunctions were expanded using a mixed Gaussian-and-plane-wave scheme with TZV2PMOLOPT basis sets[73] for all elements and a 600 Ry auxiliary plane-wave cutoff for the electron density.[74,75] Self-consistent-field convergence was accelerated using the orbital-transformation (OT)[76] and Direct Inversion in the Iterative Subspace (DIIS) algorithms,[77,78] with _ϵ_ SCF = 10 _[−]_[6] . Single-point calculations were carried out in periodic orthorhombic cells whose dimensions matched those of the molecular configurations sampled from the earlier trajectories (see Table I). The `CELL` ~~`R`~~ `EF` keyword in CP2K was used to define a fixed reference cell with a box length of 25[˚] A, ensuring consistency in the reference data used for training the MLIP, especially when cell fluctuations were present in NpT simulations. The resulting high-fidelity energy and force datasets served as the ground-truth labels for training the MLIP.

It is worth stressing that although DeepMD uses local atomic descriptors, longrange Coulomb contributions are included in the DFT reference energies and forces, allowing their effects to be learned implicitly by the MLIP. 

The DeepMD architecture contains two neural-network components: an embedding network and a fitting network as shown later in Fig. 2. The embedding network maps raw local structural information, including interatomic distances and angular correlations, into symmetry-preserving descriptors of each atomic environment. These descriptors are then passed to the fitting network, which predicts the corresponding atomic energy contributions. This construction preserves translational, rotational, and permutational invariance while allowing the model to learn many-body interactions from the DFT reference data. 

For the all-atom MLIPs, we used the Deep Potential Smooth Edition (DeepPot-SE) descriptor, `se a` , which incorporates radial and angular local information, with a cutoff radius of 6.0[˚] A and a smoothing onset of 0.5[˚] A. The embedding network contained layers of 20, 40, and 80 neurons, with an axis embedding dimension of 12, while the fitting network comprised four hidden layers of 240 neurons each. The final active-learning dataset contained 2886 DFT-labeled configurations. Each final DeepMD-kit v3.0 model was trained for 2 _._ 0 _×_ 10[6] steps with a learning rate decaying from 10 _[−]_[3] to 10 _[−]_[6] , using a decay interval of 5000 steps. The trained models were subsequently frozen and compressed for production simulations. 

## **2. Coarse-Grained Training and Simulations for All-Atom Initialization** 

To assess the convergence of Zn–Cl speciation with respect to initial configurations in subsequent MLIP-MD simulations, we developed an auxiliary ion-only coarsegrained (CG) potential using DeePMD-kit. The reference data were obtained from atomistic simulations based on revPBE without D3 dispersion corrections, for 

a 3 m aqueous ZnCl2 solution containing 26 Zn ions, 52 Cl ions, and 481 water molecules in a periodic cubic cell of approximately 25.5[˚] A per side. We downselected 25,000 configurations using uniformly spaced trajectory windows to reduce temporal correlations. Only Zn and Cl coordinates were retained, while their reference forces included interactions with all atoms, including water. Solvent effects were thus incorporated approximately through force matching. 

The CG model employed the same DeepPot-SE descriptor and neural-network architecture as the all-atom MLIPs, but with a larger cutoff radius of 12[˚] A. Training was performed for 10[6] steps using only ionic forces, with energy and virial weights set to zero. From the CGMD simulations, we selected five configurations (C2–C6) exhibiting distinct ionic structures and Zn–Cl coordination environments. These configurations were solvated with water using Packmol[82] and used to initialize independent explicit-solvent DeepMD simulations lasting 12.5–25 ns to assess the sensitivity of the predicted Zn– Cl speciation to the initial configurations. Further details and structural comparisons with all-atom simulations are provided in the Supporting Materials (SM). 

## **C. MLIP MD Simulations** 

MLIP-driven MD simulations of aqueous ZnCl2 solutions from 1 to 30 m were performed using the revPBEbased MLIPs developed in this work and a previously reported SCAN-based MLIP.[8] Production simulations at 3–30 m were performed in the _NpT_ ensemble at 298 K and 1 bar using a timestep of 0.5 fs. The _NpT_ ensemble was chosen to allow the density to equilibrate, given the greater uncertainty in solution densities at these concentrations. Simulations at 1–2 m were performed in the _NV T_ ensemble. Periodic boundary conditions were applied in all directions. Each system was simulated for approximately 20–50 ns, depending on concentration, with 50 ns trajectories used at higher concentrations. The first 10–25 ns of each trajectory were discarded as equilibration, leaving 10–25 ns of production data for the speciation and EXAFS analyses. These long trajectories are used to obtain reliable speciation statistics, with convergence demonstrated later. Representative simulation cells are summarized in Table II. 

Representative configurations from the production MLIP-MD trajectories are shown in Figure 1. At low concentrations, Zn-centered coordination environments are predominantly isolated and hydrated by water. As concentration increases, chloride coordination becomes more prevalent, and neighboring Zn centers increasingly share chloride ligands, forming more connected Zn–Cl structures. These structural changes are examined quantitatively in the Results and Discussion.

## **D. Reference AIMD simulations** 

Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations of dilute aqueous ZnCl2 systems were performed using the Quickstep module of CP2K.[66] For the coordinationnumber PMF calculations, the system contained one Zn[2+] ion, four Cl[–] ions, and 264 water molecules consistent with MLIP MD simulations: all systems were simulated in a cubic cell with a side length of 20[˚] A under periodic boundary conditions. Valence electrons were treated explicitly within density functional theory using the revised Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof functional (revPBE)[69] and the DZVP–MOLOPT–SR–GTH basis set,[73] with a plane-wave density cutoff of 400 Ha. Core electrons were represented using Goedecker–Teter– Hutter pseudopotentials,[72] and long-range dispersion interactions were included through the Grimme DFT-D3 correction.[71] 

The simulations were performed in the canonical ensemble at 300 K using a Nos´e-Hoover thermostat. The umbrella windows were separated by 0.1 in coordination number. A time step of 0.5 fs was used, and each window was simulated for 20 ps. The corresponding restraint strengths were adjusted according to the position of each window relative to the local free-energy minima. The first 2.5 ps were discarded as equilibration, and the remaining 17.5 ps were used for analysis. 

## **E. Classical MD simulations** 

To provide a comparison with the MLIP simulations, classical MD simulations of aqueous ZnCl2 solutions were performed using the LAMMPS software package[83] . Water was represented by the SPC/E model,[84] while the Zn[2+] –Cl[–] and ion–water interactions were described using the pair-specific Lennard–Jones parameters of a carefully developed and experimentally benchmarked classical force field for aqueous ZnCl2 by Stack and coworkers.[33,85] This force field was specifically refined against neutron diffraction measurements. The SPC/E O–H bonds and H–O–H angles were constrained using the SHAKE algorithm with a relative tolerance of 10 _[−]_[4] . Lennard–Jones and real-space electrostatic interactions were truncated at 9[˚] A, while long-range electrostatic interactions were evaluated using the particle–particle

## **F. Speciation Analysis** 

The populations of Zn–chloride complexes were determined from the instantaneous first coordination shell of each Zn ion. For every trajectory frame, a molecular graph was constructed by assigning Zn–Cl and Zn– O contacts when the corresponding interatomic distance was less than 3 _._ 0[˚] A. An O–H cutoff of 1 _._ 2[˚] A was used to preserve the identity of individual water molecules. Each Zn ion was then classified according to the numbers of directly coordinated chloride ions, _n_ , and water molecules, _m_ , and the resulting species were denoted as [ZnCln(H2O)m][2–n] . Species populations were obtained by counting the occurrence of each coordination-shell composition over all Zn ions and trajectory frames and normalizing by the total number of Zn-ion–frame observations. This procedure provides the relative population of each Zn coordination environment at a given electrolyte concentration. ## **F. Classical MD versus Universal and Custom MLIPs** 

Beyond agreement with EXAFS, the accuracy of the underlying interaction model and adequate sampling of Zn–Cl coordination environments are important considerations. As discussed in Sec. III B, CMD speciation populations converge more slowly than those from DPMDSCAN: independent CMD trajectories retain noticeable differences after 50 ns, whereas the dominant DeepMD species populations become largely independent of their initial configurations within 25 ns. Although the CMD speciation populations are not fully converged, we compare the corresponding EXAFS spectra with those obtained from MLIP-MD as an approximate assessment of the CMD predictions, acknowledging the remaining sampling uncertainty. 

Figure 11 compares the EXAFS spectra obtained from the three simulation approaches at 3 m. The same FEFF workflow was used for all three ensembles, so differences among the calculated spectra reflect the structures sampled by the simulations. In _k_ space, both DPMD-SCAN and MACE-MATPES-r2SCAN reproduce the principal experimental oscillations reasonably well in phase and amplitude. In contrast, CMD substantially underestimates the oscillation amplitude and exhibits differences in phase and oscillation envelope. Similarly, in Fouriertransform space, both MLIPs reproduce the dominant first-shell feature more closely than CMD, which predicts a considerably weaker peak. 

The corresponding Zn–O and Zn–Cl RDFs and running coordination numbers in Figure 12 reveal substantial differences in local coordination. CMD predicts fewer first-shell Zn–O neighbors (approximately 2.7 versus 3.5– 4.0 for the MLIPs) and more Zn–Cl neighbors (approximately 1.9 versus 1.3). ## **IV. SUMMARY & CONCLUSIONS** 

This work addresses closely related challenges in concentrated aqueous electrolytes: the transferability of MLIPs across concentrations, the sensitivity of EXAFS to underlying speciation, and the convergence of coordination populations in MD simulations. To investigate these questions, we combine experimental Zn K-edge EXAFS with long-timescale MD simulations of aqueous ZnCl2, using two revPBE-based DeepMD potentials developed here and a previously available SCAN-based potential, together with comparisons to a universal MLIP and CMD. 

We first benchmarked the EXAFS calculations against two well-defined reference complexes, [Zn(H2O)6][2+] and [ZnCl4][2–] , and then calculated ensemble-averaged EXAFS spectra from thousands of Zn-centered MD configurations. Among the models examined, DPMD-SCAN provides the most consistent agreement with experimental EXAFS, reproducing the spectral evolution from dilute to concentrated ZnCl2 solutions. SCAN-based simulations also sample local coordination environments whose calculated spectra capture the main features of mixed ZnCl2/LiCl measurements. This performance supports its greater transferability across ZnCl2 concentrations without retraining at each composition. The MD simulations capture the progressive replacement of water by chloride in the first coordination shell and the increasing importance of chloride-bridged Zn structures at higher concentrations. These trends are consistent with previous SCAN-based simulations and experimen- 

18 

tal XANES observations.[8,63] In contrast, the revPBED3-based potential reproduces EXAFS at selected concentrations, particularly 3 m, but generally overestimates chloride coordination and provides less consistent agreement across concentrations. Removing the Zn-related D3 contributions does not systematically resolve these discrepancies, highlighting the sensitivity of predicted speciation to the underlying DFT description. Importantly, at 3 m, substantially different coordination environments produce similar ensemble-averaged EXAFS spectra, demonstrating that spectral agreement does not uniquely constrain Zn speciation, particularly the populations of isolated and chloride-bridged complexes. 

Significant discrepancies with experiment can reveal limitations of the underlying interaction models. At 3 m, CMD shows substantially larger deviations from experimental EXAFS than DPMD-SCAN or MACE-MATPESr2SCAN, predicting fewer Zn–O and more Zn–Cl neighbors. Despite narrower neighbor-distance distributions, CMD produces weaker EXAFS oscillations, indicating that radial disorder alone cannot explain the spectral differences. However, incomplete equilibration introduces uncertainty in its predicted coordination populations. The universal MACE-MATPES-r2SCAN potential performs considerably better than CMD and produces EXAFS spectra broadly comparable to DPMDSCAN at 3 m, although this agreement does not establish the accuracy of its speciation predictions or transferability across concentrations. DeepMD is approximately 50 times faster than MACE for a 30[˚] A simulation box, although CMD remains considerably faster than both MLIPs. Considering its agreement with experiment across ZnCl2 concentrations and substantially lower computational cost than MACE, DPMD-SCAN offers a favorable balance. 

Convergence tests using independent initial configurations demonstrate that the dominant DeepMD coordination populations become largely independent of their initial configurations within 25 ns, whereas CMD retains substantial differences even after longer simulations. This slower convergence is consistent with kinetic trapping associated with strong fixed point-charge electrostatic interactions, whereas DeepMD learns electrostatic effects implicitly from DFT reference energies and forces without imposing fixed ionic charges.[13,27,28,91–94] Even with DeepMD, less abundant multinuclear aggregates remain more difficult to sample. In future work, we will present related enhanced-sampling approaches to calculate EXAFS spectra across concentrations, reducing the need for long unbiased MD simulations. 

The importance of accurate interaction models and adequate sampling becomes especially clear when examining the concentration dependence of Zn coordination and connectivity. As ZnCl2 concentration increases, octahedral Zn environments become less prevalent, while chloride coordination and bridging increase. DPMD-SCAN better reproduces the available experimental trends: at 30 m, its predicted octahedral fraction (19%) is close to 

the XANES estimate (21%), while average Zn–Cl coordination approaches three, consistent with chloride sharing. In contrast, CMD shows less consistent agreement with the XANES-derived octahedral fractions, overestimating them at 1 m and underestimating them at higher concentrations. The predicted extent of aggregation also remains strongly model-dependent. At 10 m, revPBEwo-D3-on-Zn predicts approximately 78% of Zn in aggregates containing three or more Zn centers, compared with less than 1% for SCAN. These differences underscore why local coordination and molecular connectivity must be analyzed separately. 

These contrasting structural predictions also clarify what EXAFS can establish about electrolyte speciation. Our neighbor-resolved analysis shows that distinct coordination ensembles can produce similar EXAFS spectra because of structural disorder, variations in neighbor distances, and interference among scattering paths. Thus, EXAFS provides important constraints on local Zn coordination but does not uniquely determine the populations of isolated and chloride-bridged complexes. The agreement of DPMD-SCAN with EXAFS across concentrations, together with independent XANES observations, strengthens confidence in its description of local structure without uniquely validating its predicted speciation. Reliable speciation predictions therefore require accurate interatomic interactions, converged molecular sampling, ensemble-averaged spectral calculations, and explicit analysis of coordination and connectivity. Overall, these findings establish the importance of assessing MLIP transferability across concentrations and combining rigorous molecular simulations with complementary experimental constraints for predictive speciation in concentrated electrolytes.
