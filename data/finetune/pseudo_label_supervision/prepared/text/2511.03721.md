Dirac semimetals of the form _A_ Mn _X_ 2 ( _A_ = alkaline-earth or divalent rare earth; _X_ = Bi, Sb) host conducting square-net Dirac-electron layers of _X_ atoms interleaved with antiferromagnetic Mn _X_ layers. Hypervalent pnictogen square nets have long been predicted to host an electronically driven Peierls instability, whereas low-temperature anomalies in CaMnBi2 near _T[∗] ∼_ 50 K have previously been discussed in terms of possible magnetic canting, TRS breaking, and a resulting Weyl-semimetal (WSM) state. Here we use polarized and unpolarized neutron diffraction, x-ray diffraction, and density functional theory (DFT) to investigate this transition in single-crystal CaMnBi2. We observe a coupled structural/magnetic symmetry-lowering transition at _T[∗]_ = 46(2) K from a tetragonal lattice with C-type antiferromagnetism to an orthorhombic phase with unit-cell doubling along the _c_ axis, minimal impact on magnetism, and no measurable uniform spin canting (≲ 2 _[◦]_ ). Superlattice peak intensities and the lattice distortion reveal a continuous second-order transition governed by a single primary order parameter. The refined displacement pattern corresponds to a zigzag bond-order-wave (BOW) modulation of in-plane Bi–Bi bonds, providing direct diffraction evidence for a Peierls-type instability of the Dirac-electron Bi layer anticipated by Hoffmann and co-workers [W. Tremel and R. Hoffmann, _J. Am. Chem. Soc._ **109** , 124 (1987); G. A. Papoian and R. Hoffmann, _Angew. Chem. Int. Ed._ **39** , 2408 (2000)]. These results identify the _T[∗]_ anomaly as an intrinsic Dirac-electron-driven Peierls/BOW transition of the Bi square net. 

## **I. INTRODUCTION** 

Extensive research on topological quantum materials (TQMs) over the past decade has uncovered a range of novel electronic phenomena, providing platforms for controlling symmetry, topology, and electronic order, with potential technological applications in quantum computation, infrared sensors, chemical catalysis, and spintronics [1–5]. The initial impetus came from the discovery of Dirac fermions in graphene [5, 6], which spurred searches for Dirac-like quasiparticles and topological band structures in layered quasi-two-dimensional (2D) and bulk three-dimensional (3D) materials. Beyond topology itself, an increasingly important theme is how these Dirac-derived electronic states couple to collective instabilities, including magnetism, density-wave, bond-order, and nematic distortions, that lower crystal symmetry and reconstruct the low-energy bands.

One particularly interesting class of TQMs is topological semimetals (TSMs), which are defined by symmetry-protected band touching points (nodes) in the vicinity of the Fermi energy and can be considered a 3D analogue of the prototypical TQM graphene [2, 7– 10]. Depending on the character of the topological band crossing, TSMs are further classified into different families. The point-like crossing of spin-degenerate bands gives rise to four-fold degenerate Dirac nodes in Dirac semimetals (DSMs), while lifting the spin degeneracy can yield two-fold crossing points of spin-split bands in Weyl semimetals (WSMs). Because the low-energy electronic structure is controlled by symmetry-protected crossings with a low density of states (DOS), even subtle symmetry breaking can qualitatively reconstruct Dirac states, making TSMs a natural platform for electronically driven instabilities. 

One such remarkable electronic instability is the Peierls transition, originally predicted by Peierls for one-dimensional metals at half-filling, leading to an intrinsic breakdown of the metallic state [11]. Later, similar Peierls instabilities were predicted for Dirac electrons in pnictogen square nets near half-filling [12, 13]. This instability is remarkable because at its core it is entirely kinematic and thus profoundly generic: it relies only on the Pauli exclusion principle and electron-lattice coupling and requires no interaction between the mobile 

2 

electrons. At half-filling, a distortion doubling the unit cell opens a gap at the Fermi level via bond modulation and lowers the energy of the filled band, yielding an insulating state. An analogous mechanism underlies spin-Peierls dimerization in spin-1/2 chains, describable in terms of neutral Jordan-Wigner fermions [14]. This differs markedly from a charge-density-wave instability driven primarily by electron-electron Coulomb interactions. For two-dimensional Dirac electrons, several bond-order patterns reconstructing the Fermi surface were predicted [12, 13]. 

On the other hand, a Weyl semimetallic state (WSS), hosting Weyl fermions, the chiral anomaly, and Fermi arcs, can arise when a Dirac semimetal is perturbed by breaking either spatial inversion symmetry (IS) or time-reversal symmetry (TRS), yielding spin-split bands [5, 15]. The latter route is often sought in magnetic topological semimetals. At the same time, symmetry lowering driven by electronic and electron-lattice interactions, such as density-wave or Peierls bond-order instabilities, provides an alternative and broadly relevant mechanism for reconstructing Dirac bands without TRS breaking or a net ferromagnetic moment, and can, in principle, compete or intertwine with WSS physics. 

The 112 ternary pnictides with general formula _A_ Mn _X_ 2 ( _A_ = alkaline-earth or divalent rare-earth metal; _X_ = Bi, Sb) provide an exemplary magnetic TSM platform [7, 16–20]. Their crystal structure comprises square-net layers of _X_ atoms hosting topological Dirac fermions, interleaved with Mn _X_ planes and _A_ cation layers [Fig. 1]. The Mn atoms form a square lattice with antiferromagnetic (AFM) order near room temperature [Fig. 1(b)]. In this family, weak ferromagnetism arising from uniform canting of Mn moments can generate a TRS-breaking in-plane ferromagnetic (FM) moment and has been discussed as a route to a type-II Weyl state. However, the same square-net chemistry also renders these materials susceptible to Peierls-type reconstruction of the Dirac bands associated with an electronically driven bond disproportionation in the _X_ layers that lowers the crystal symmetry and reconstructs the low-energy electronic structure [12, 13]. 

In CaMnBi2, which we study here, an unexpected transition was revealed by measurements of the in-plane resistivity, which shows a cusp with a slight upturn upon cooling near 50 K [21, 22]. An optical study reported a decrease in the Drude weight below this transition [23], while magnetic torque measurements, combined with _ab initio_ DFT calculations and analysis of the optical response were interpreted as evidence for a _∼_ 10 _[◦]_ in-plane canting of the Mn moments [23]. Testing this hypothesis and resolving the origin of the _T[∗]_ anomaly were the original motivations for the present study; as we show below, the data instead point to an intrinsic Dirac-electron-driven symmetry-lowering instability of the Bi square-net layer. 

Central to our results and to the electronic behavior 

of the 112 family more broadly is the Peierls-type instability of hypervalent pnictogen square nets analyzed by Hoffmann and collaborators [12, 13]. For a square net of _X_ (Sb or Bi) atoms, the electronic states near the Fermi level are dominated by bands derived from the nearly half-filled _px_ and _py_ orbitals; their crossings form four symmetry-related Dirac cones [Fig. 1(d)]. When the Fermi energy lies at or near these Dirac nodes, close to half filling, a Peierls/BOW distortion can lower the energy of the occupied electronic states by gapping the Dirac points. In CaMnBi2, this distortion shifts the centering Bi atoms, producing a zigzag modulation of the in-plane Bi–Bi bonds and lowering the lattice symmetry from tetragonal to orthorhombic; the equilibrium displacement reflects the balance between electronic energy gain and the cost of lattice elastic strain. This bond modulation enables hybridization of the _px_ and _py_ bands and reconstructs the Dirac bands by gapping two of the four Dirac cones [Fig. 1(e)], providing a natural microscopic origin for the partial Fermi-surface gapping associated with the anomaly at _T[∗]_ . 

While Tremel and Hoffmann [12] and Papoian and Hoffmann [13] cited SrZnSb2 as the only example of Peierls-distorted orthorhombic structure with zig-zag Sb chains, recent studies of _A_ MnSb2 compounds have identified similar distortions in many other cases: _A_ = Sr [24–26], Ca [27–30], Ba [31–33], Eu [34], and Yb [35], correcting earlier reports of the undistorted tetragonal structure with ideal square nets for _A_ = Ba [36, 37] and Yb [19, 38]. Moreover, the zigzag distortion was found to persist even in a high-entropy mixed-anion compound, Ba0 _._ 38Sr0 _._ 14Ca0 _._ 16Eu0 _._ 16Yb0 _._ 16MnSb2 [39], showing that the Peierls-distorted orthorhombic structure is robust and common among Sb compounds of the 112 family. 

On the other hand, no structural distortion has been observed in YbMnBi2 [16, 18, 40], EuMnBi2 [4, 41], SrMnBi2, or CaMnBi2 [17, 28], all of which were previously reported to retain the ideal square-net tetragonal ( _P_ 4 _/nmm_ ) structure. These results indicate that whereas the zigzag BOW motif associated with the square-net Peierls tendency is robust and widespread in Sb-based 112 compounds, the corresponding instability is weaker in Bi-based members and may emerge only as a low-temperature, electronically driven transition. 

This absence of distortion in Bi compounds is, in principle, consistent with a reduced Peierls instability in the heavier pnictogen (Bi) layers. As discussed in Ref. 13, this trend can be rationalized by weaker _sp_ hybridization in higher- _Z_ elements, which suppresses the electronic instability; the same analysis also predicted that a less electronegative _A_ -site cation further disfavors distortion. Nevertheless, a slight structural distortion was recently reported in BaMnBi2, with an orthorhombicity about 10% of that in BaMnSb2 [42], revising earlier reports of an undistorted square-net configuration [43].

Our results establish a direct experimental link between square-net Dirac semimetals and Peierls/BOW physics, providing an electronically driven, compositionally tunable route to symmetry breaking and low-energy electronic band reconstruction 

in a widely studied topological materials family.

## **II. STRUCTURE AND PHYSICAL PROPERTIES OF CaMnBi** 2 

CaMnBi2 is a Dirac semimetal characterized by strongly anisotropic Dirac dispersion, with Dirac points located near the Fermi energy [21, 44]. Its crystal structure, with tetragonal _P_ 4 _/nmm_ symmetry at room temperature, was first reported in 1980 based on measurements on a polycrystalline sample [45]. All recent measurements on both single-crystal and powder samples at room temperature confirm the structure to be _P_ 4 _/nmm_ [16, 17, 21, 22, 46]. For this crystal structure, 

4 

Bragg reflections of the ( _H, K,_ 0) type are allowed for _H_ + _K_ = 2 _n_ , and of the ( _H,_ 0 _,_ 0) type for _H_ = 2 _n_ , where _n_ is an integer. 

In-plane electronic transport measurements [17, 21, 22] reveal metallic behavior, but with a relatively high in-plane residual resistivity of ≳ 20 _µ_ Ω _·_ cm at _T →_ 0 K, indicating that CaMnBi2 is a moderately bad metal [22]. The temperature dependence of magnetic susceptibility, _χ_ ( _T_ ), exhibits a feature associated with AFM ordering, with a reported N´eel temperature _T_ N ranging from 250 K to 300 K across different studies [17, 21, 22]. No clear signatures of the AFM transition are observed in resistivity measurements. However, at a lower temperature around _T[∗] ∼_ 50 K, Refs. 21 and 22 reported a hump- or cusp-like anomaly in resistivity, interpreted as a manifestation of Fermi surface instability and reconstruction of the Fermi surface resulting from uniform spin canting [22, 23, 47]. A subtle, plateau-like feature in _χ_ ( _T_ ) is also observed near _T[∗]_ [22]. It was also found that Na substitution at the Ca site enhances the resistive anomaly, with _T[∗]_ in Ca1 _−x_ Na _x_ MnBi2 increasing to _≈_ 75 K and _≈_ 100 K for 5% and 9% Na substitution, respectively [23, 47]. 

Previous neutron powder diffraction measurements on a polycrystalline sample of CaMnBi2 identified a N´eel-type (also known as C-type) AFM ordering described by the _P_ 4 _[′] /n[′] m[′] m_ magnetic space group below _T_ N _≈_ 300 K, with an ordered Mn magnetic moment of _µ_ Mn _≈_ 3 _._ 7 _µB_ at _T_ = 10 K [17]. In this magnetic structure, Mn moments are aligned along the easy _c_ -axis with the nearest-neighbor moments in the _ab_ -plane aligned antiferromagnetically and those along the _c_ -axis aligned ferromagnetically. The same magnetic structure and ordered moment were later confirmed in a single-crystal diffraction study, although a slightly lower N´eel temperature of _T_ N _≈_ 264 K was reported [48]. The magnetic propagation vector is _**τ**_ = (1 _,_ 0 _,_ 0); thus, for a crystal aligned in the ( _H,_ 0 _, L_ ) horizontal scattering plane, magnetic Bragg peaks are expected at reciprocal lattice vectors of the form (1 + 2 _n,_ 0 _, m_ ), where _n_ and _m_ are integers [48, 49]. 

If the antiferromagnetic structure were uniformly canted, producing a ferromagnetic component in the _ab_ plane, magnetic intensity should appear at these and other nuclear Bragg positions. Although this possibility was not specifically investigated, previous neutron-diffraction studies reported no evidence of the putative spin canting inferred from bulk measurements in the low-temperature magnetic structure. 

The implications of spin canting and the resulting in-plane ferromagnetism are intriguing, as they raise the possibility of a Weyl semimetallic state in CaMnBi2 or its doped variants. However, the magnetic torque measurements from which a surprisingly large _∼_ 10 _[◦]_ spin canting was inferred in Ref. 23 were performed on a bulk sample and are therefore susceptible to contributions from ferromagnetic inclusions, impurity phases, or crystal imperfections. Indeed, the authors observed 

ferromagnetic moments of comparable magnitude both in the _ab_ -plane and along the _c_ -axis, attributing the latter to the presence of vacancies [23, 50]. Moreover, torque measurements are sensitive to magnetic field misalignment, which can introduce significant bias given the subtle effect of the putative in-plane FM moment on the measured angular dependence of magnetic torque [23]. Finally, the measurements were conducted in applied magnetic fields of a few Tesla, which may enhance misalignment effects and could also modify the zero-field magnetic structure, potentially leading to incorrect conclusions about canted magnetism. 

Neutron diffraction measurements are essential for determining whether spin canting and the resulting weak ferromagnetism appear in CaMnBi2 below the resistive-anomaly temperature _T[∗]_ . A canting angle of _∼_ 10 _[◦]_ , as reported in Ref. 23, would be readily detectable by neutron scattering [40, 49]. Here, we use single-crystal neutron diffraction to track the evolution of the magnetic and lattice structures of CaMnBi2 across _T[∗]_ . While our data rule out the large canting and weak ferromagnetism inferred from bulk torque measurements, they reveal an intriguing structural transition: the Bi square net undergoes a distortion that breaks it into zigzag chains, consistent with the two-dimensional Peierls-type bond-/charge-density-wave (BOW/CDW) predicted by Hoffmann and co-workers [12, 13], as illustrated in Fig. 1. 

## **III. EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS** 

Single crystals of CaMnBi2 were grown using a high-temperature self-flux method, following the procedure described in Ref. 21. Stoichiometric mixtures of Ca (99.99%), Mn (99.9%), and excess Bi (99.99%) with a ratio of Ca:Mn:Bi = 1:1:9 were sealed in a quartz tube, heated to 1050 _[◦]_ C, and then cooled to 450 _[◦]_ C, where the crystals were decanted. The resultant crystals are plate-like and the basal plane of a cleaved crystal is the crystallographic _ab_ plane. Electrical transport measurements were performed using a conventional four-wire method in a Quantum Design Physical Property Measurement System (PPMS-9). Magnetization measurements were carried out in a Quantum Design Magnetic Property Measurement System (MPMS). 

For neutron diffraction measurements, a 160 mg plate-like crystal was mounted on an aluminum plate and aligned in the ( _H,_ 0 _, L_ ) horizontal scattering plane. The sample holder was attached to the cold finger of a closed-cycle cryostat, enabling temperature control from 5 to 315 K. Unpolarized neutron scattering measurements were performed on the SPINS triple-axis spectrometer at the NIST Center for Neutron Research (NCNR) with a fixed final energy of _E_ f = 5 meV and horizontal beam collimations of G–80 _[′]_ –S–80 _[′]_ –open (G = guide, S = sample). Two beryllium filters, placed before and after the sample, were used to suppress higher-order 

5 

contamination from the monochromator. 

Polarized neutron diffraction measurements were carried out on the HB-1 thermal neutron triple-axis spectrometer (TAS) at the High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR), Oak Ridge National Laboratory, using the same crystal previously measured with unpolarized neutrons. A Heusler monochromator and analyzer were employed for polarization analysis, and measurements were conducted in the **Q** _∥_ **P** configuration, where **Q** is the wave vector transfer and **P** is the neutron polarization. The horizontal beam collimations were 48 _[′]_ -80 _[′]_ -S-60 _[′]_ -240 _[′]_ , with a fixed incident neutron energy of 13.5 meV. Contamination from higher-order reflections was effectively suppressed using pyrolytic graphite (PG) filters. A flipping ratio of 16 was measured at nuclear Bragg reflections. 

For improved structural refinement, additional unpolarized neutron diffraction measurements were performed in four-circle mode on the DEMAND (HB-3A) diffractometer at HFIR. The same crystal previously used in the triple-axis measurements was aligned in the ( _H, K,_ 0) horizontal scattering plane, and neutrons with a wavelength of 1.003[˚] A, selected by a Si (331) monochromator, were used. Magnetic and nuclear structures were refined using the FullProf Suite. 

Single-crystal x-ray diffraction measurements were performed on a four-circle diffractometer using Cu _Kα_ 1 radiation from a rotating anode x-ray source at Ames National Laboratory, using a germanium (111) monochromator. Measurements were conducted on a 100 mg single crystal mounted on a flat copper sample holder, which was attached to the cold finger of a closed-cycle helium refrigerator to enable temperature-dependent studies. Three beryllium domes were used as the vacuum shroud, heat shield, and sample enclosure. The innermost dome, which housed the sample, was filled with a small amount of helium exchange gas to improve thermal contact. 

Except for the low-temperature data used in the refinement of the _Pcmn_ ( _Pnma_ ) crystal structure, most of the single-crystal data—including that shown in the figures—are indexed using the high-temperature tetragonal _P_ 4 _/nmm_ lattice. The momentum transfer, **Q** = ( _H, K, L_ ) = (2 _π/a_ ) _H_[ˆ] **i** + (2 _π/b_ ) _K_[ˆ] **j** + (2 _π/c_ ) _L_ **k**[ˆ] , is expressed in this setting, with unit cell parameters _a_ = 4 _._ 50[˚] A and _c_ = 11 _._ 07[˚] A [17, 48].

## **B. N´eel-type Antiferromagnetic Order** 

As noted above, magnetic Bragg peaks corresponding to C-type AFM order in CaMnBi2 are expected at (1 + 2 _n,_ 0 _, m_ ) reciprocal lattice positions, where _n_ and _m_ are integers. Figures 3(a) and (b) show that the (1, 0, 0) peak, where nuclear scattering is forbidden, emerges below _T_ N in both the unpolarized and spin-flip (SF) channels of the polarized neutron measurements. Enhanced intensity was also observed at other (1 _,_ 0 _, L_ ) reflections, which contain both nuclear and magnetic scattering contributions. No magnetic signal was detected below _T_ N at (0 _,_ 0 _, L_ )-type reflections, consistent with Mn moments aligned along the _c_ -axis (see also the discussion in Sec. IV C). The temperature dependence of the integrated intensity of the (1, 0, 0) peak from both polarized and unpolarized measurements is shown in Fig. 3(c). A power-law fit to the data near the transition yields _T_ N = 270(1) K and a critical exponent _β_ = 0 _._ 31(3), close to the value _β ≈_ 0 _._ 327 for the three-dimensional (3D) Ising model.

## **G. Symmetry of the** _Pcmn_ **Distortion** 

As discussed in Section IV E and Appendix D, only the orthorhombic _Pcmn_ subgroup of the high-temperature tetragonal _P_ 4 _/nmm_ structure permits atomic displacements consistent with both the observed superlattice peaks and a continuous structural transition. This subgroup contains two irreducible representations (IRs) of the parent _P_ 4 _/nmm_ space group, _Z_ 5 _[−]_[and] _[Z]_ 5[+][,][which][differ][by][an][origin][shift][of][the] _Pcmn_ unit cell by (0, 0, 0) and (0, 0, 1/2), respectively, relative to the _P_ 4 _/nmm_ lattice. In the _Z_ 5 _[−]_ case, Bi1 atoms in the square-net layers at _z_ = 0 undergo anti-phase displacements, producing bond disproportionation and zig-zag Bi chains staggered between adjacent Bi1 layers, leading to lattice doubling. Thus, the refined displacements directly produce alternating in-plane Bi–Bi bonds, forming the zigzag BOW. This bond modulation is the structural ingredient of the Peierls mechanism that reconstructs the Bi-derived Dirac bands, opening a gap along the dimerization direction while preserving the Dirac nodes along the perpendicular zigzag direction [Fig. 1(e)]. For the intra-unit-cell Bi2, Mn, and Ca layers, in-phase, shear-type displacements are allowed [Fig. 9(a),(b)]. This structure corresponds to the Peierls-type distortion of Bi layers proposed by Hoffmann and co-workers [12, 13]. Conversely, in the _Z_ 5[+][case,][zig-zag][chains][form][in][the] Mn layers at _z_ = 0 and _z_ = 0 _._ 5 of the doubled _Pcmn_ 

unit cell, while other intra-cell layers undergo shear-type displacements. The resulting in-plane Mn-Mn bond order [Fig. 9(c)] is similar to the bond-order wave associated with ferro-orbital ordering observed in the Fe layers of FeTe [61].

## **A. Total Energy of Competing Lattice Distortions** 

To evaluate the relative stability of different lattice distortion modes in CaMnBi2, we performed total energy calculations for several candidate structures, including the high-symmetry tetragonal _P_ 4 _/nmm_ phase and symmetry-lowered orthorhombic configurations corresponding to the irreducible representations _Z_ 5 _[−]_ and _Z_ 5[+][.] These distorted structures were generated by introducing displacement patterns obtained from ISODISTORT analysis and were subsequently relaxed under symmetry constraints. The resulting total energies were then compared to identify the energetically preferred distortion mode and to estimate the driving force behind the structural phase transition at _T[∗]_ . All calculations were carried out at zero temperature and fixed experimental volume, unless otherwise specified, similar to the analysis of EuZnSb2 [67]. 

Table III summarizes the total energies and lattice parameters for the tetragonal and various orthorhombic phases, based on both experimentally refined and DFT-relaxed structures. When comparing the experimentally refined orthorhombic structures corresponding to the _Z_ 5 _[−]_[(dimerization][in][the][Bi][layer)] and _Z_ 5[+] (dimerization in the Mn layer) irreducible representations, the _Z_ 5 _[−]_ phase is found to be slightly lower in energy. However, in full lattice relaxation calculations initiated with varying dimerization amplitudes, both the _Z_ 5 _[−]_ and _Z_ 5[+] configurations ultimately relax back to the high-symmetry tetragonal phase. 

We further performed total energy calculations exploring the space of shear and dimerization amplitudes. Figure 22(a) in Appendix I shows the energy cost for varying dimerization in the Mn and Bi layers, without introducing shear distortion. While dimerization in the Bi square layer is slightly more favorable energetically than in the Mn layer, consistent with our earlier result that the _Z_ 5 _[−]_[structure][is][slightly][lower][in][energy] than the _Z_ 5[+][structure][(Table][III),][both][configurations] correspond to higher-energy states in our calculations. Figures 22(b) and 22(c) in Appendix I present the total energy landscape as a function of both dimerization and shear distortions for the _Z_ 5 _[−]_ and _Z_ 5[+] cases, respectively. In our DFT analysis, the minimum total 

energy always corresponds to the undistorted tetragonal phase, indicating that the energy gain associated with the distortion is very small and within the uncertainty of the DFT calculations. This result is not unexpected, given that the transition temperature _T[∗]_ is only _≈_ 46 K, corresponding to a thermal energy scale of _≈_ 4 meV.

Consistent with previous studies, the Bi 5 _p_ states near the Fermi level, originating from the square-net Bi atoms, give rise to Dirac nodal points at _≈−_ 0 _._ 3 eV. 

To understand the impact of BOW dimerization on the electronic structure, we then compared the DOS for the dimerized phases [Fig. 23(b) in Appendix I]. The DOS profiles for the experimentally refined _Z_ 5 _[−]_ and _Z_ 5[+] distortions are nearly identical, as expected given the small DOS of Dirac nodes and very small experimental dimerization amplitude ( _∼_ 10 _[−]_[3][˚] A). ## **VI. DISCUSSION** 

The relevance of weak ferromagnetism in _A_ Mn _X_ 2 compounds has been controversial. In SrMnSb2, crystals with measured deficiencies of Sr and Mn were reported to exhibit a FM moment that scales with the deficiency level [50]. However, neutron scattering on samples from the same group concluded that the FM order occurs as a minority phase, while the majority phase is 

antiferromagnetic with no evidence of spin canting [25]; later inelastic neutron scattering similarly found that the spin dynamics are well described by a purely AFM model, with no ferromagnetic component [26, 68]. A separate group demonstrated that SrMnSb2 crystals can be grown with no measurable FM moment [69]; furthermore, a powder diffraction study showed that heating above _∼_ 500 K leads to the appearance of small amounts of SrO and Sb, as well as a ferromagnetic response, suggesting a link to sample decomposition. 

A similar controversy has emerged in YbMnBi2 [16]. Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy combined with magneto-optical microscopy reported evidence for TRS breaking at the crystal surface, interpreted in terms of an _∼_ 10 _[◦]_ canting of Mn moments [41]. TRS breaking was also inferred from a large anomalous Nernst signal in a crystal with a weak FM moment; however, the implied canting angle was only _∼_ 0 _._ 02 _[◦]_ [70], effectively indistinguishable from zero within realistic material constraints. Neutron diffraction measurements [40, 49] found no evidence for the spin canting proposed in Ref. 41, calling into question a bulk Weyl semimetal state in this material. Optical-conductivity studies have 

15 

likewise disagreed on whether Weyl fermions are essential [71] or not [72] for interpreting the data. 

Taken together, these results highlight that bulk TRS breaking via magnetic moment canting is difficult to establish unambiguously in the 112 family. This motivates direct diffraction tests [19, 40], but also raises the possibility that some reported low-temperature anomalies instead originate from distinct intrinsic symmetry-lowering instabilities of the square-net Dirac layers, as we find here. 

In CaMnBi2, the transition at _T[∗]_ = 46(2) K corresponds to a coupled structural and magnetic symmetry lowering consistent with a Peierls-type BOW/CDW distortion in the Dirac-electron Bi layer, as originally predicted by Hoffmann and co-workers [12, 13]. This transition is manifested by the appearance below _T[∗]_ of ( _H,_ 0 _, L_ + 2[1][)][superlattice][Bragg][reflections][with] integer _H_ = 0 and _L_ (indexed with respect to the parent _P_ 4 _/nmm_ cell) in both neutron and x-ray diffraction, together with orthorhombic splitting of the fundamental ( _H,_ 0 _, L_ ) _/_ (0 _, H, L_ ) peaks in x-ray diffraction; the twinned reflections separate in the orthorhombic phase. The superlattice peaks contain comparable nuclear and magnetic contributions, consistent with anti-phase shearing displacements of the magnetic layers. 

Overall, CaMnBi2 exhibits three distinct phases in terms of magnetic and crystal structure: a high-temperature tetragonal paramagnetic phase ( _T > T_ N), a tetragonal C-type antiferromagnetic phase ( _T[∗] < T < T_ N), and a low-temperature orthorhombic Peierls/BOW phase with little impact on C-type antiferromagnetism ( _T < T[∗]_ ) [Fig. 1]. Although the orthorhombic symmetry in principle allows spin canting and the associated TRS breaking that could support a Weyl state at _T < T[∗]_ , any such weak canting, if present, is beyond our experimental detection limit. Rietveld refinements of the polarized and unpolarized neutron diffraction data show that below _T[∗]_ , CaMnBi2 transforms from the high-temperature tetragonal _P_ 4 _/nmm_ lattice with magnetic space group _P_ 4 _[′] /n[′] m[′] m_ to an orthorhombic _Pcmn_ lattice with magnetic space group _Pc[′] m[′] n[′]_ . Between the two candidate irreducible representations, _Z_ 5 _[−]_ and _Z_ 5[+][,][of][the][low-temperature] orthorhombic structure compatible with the symmetry constraints of the observed _P_ 4 _/nmm → Pcmn_ transition, the _Z_ 5 _[−]_ mode, corresponding to a zigzag BOW in the Bi1 Dirac layer, is consistently, though modestly, favored by the neutron refinements. DFT likewise finds a slightly lower energy for the _Z_ 5 _[−]_ configuration; however, the displacement amplitudes are near the limit of theoretical accuracy, and the undistorted tetragonal _P_ 4 _/nmm_ structure remains the global minimum. The _Z_ 5 _[−]_ IR, corresponding to a Peierls-type BOW/CDW distortion in the Dirac-electron Bi1 layer [Fig. 9(a),(b)], is further supported by several experimental observations. 

Although the superlattice reflections observed by polarized neutron diffraction contain comparable 

structural and magnetic components, they develop continuously through a second-order phase transition, as evidenced by the similar order-parameter-like temperature dependence of both the superlattice peak intensities and the lattice distortion parameter [Figs. 5, 6, and 8]. This behavior indicates a single primary order parameter. While we do not have enough data within a narrow critical range of _∼_ 10% of _Tc_ to reliably evaluate the critical behavior, making the extracted critical exponents somewhat phenomenological, analyses using variable fitting ranges (Appendix C) consistently yield small exponents, _β ∼_ 0 _._ 2, indicative of a quasi-two-dimensional character of the observed Peierls transition. The small critical exponent is compatible with 2D Ising-like critical behavior and supports an electronically driven 2D Peierls/BOW transition in a layered system, which can be mapped onto an effective quasi-2D Ising model [59, 60]. This contrasts with the 3D critical behavior of the antiferromagnetic order parameter at _T_ N shown in Fig. 3. An electronic Peierls mechanism illustrated in Fig. 1(d),(e) is also consistent with the partial Fermi-surface gapping inferred from optical-reflectivity measurements [23, 47] and reproduced in our DFT calculations [Fig. 23]. 

In the _Z_ 5[+][model, which involves a BOW in the Mn–Bi] layer, the magnetic contribution to the superlattice peaks arises solely from spin canting. This scenario would involve both lattice and magnetic symmetry breaking and hence two coupled order parameters, implying a first-order phase transition. Indeed, such a BOW has been observed in FeTe, where orbital dimerization in the Fe-Te layer occurs through a first-order phase transition and produces pronounced changes in Fe magnetism, manifested by a substantial change in Fe magnetic moment and magnetic susceptibility [60, 61, 73]. In contrast, Mn magnetism in CaMnBi2 appears largely insensitive to the transition at _T[∗]_ . The refined magnetic moment at 50 K, _µ_ Mn( _z_ ) = 3 _._ 68(5) _µB_ , is only slightly smaller than at 5 K, _µ_ Mn( _z_ ) = 3 _._ 97(2) _µB_ , consistent with the expected temperature evolution of the magnetic order parameter, and the magnetic susceptibility shows no discernible anomaly at _T[∗]_ [Fig. 2(a)]. 

Finally, a similar structural distortion with a zigzag BOW corresponding to the _Z_ 5 _[−]_ IR of the orthorhombic group, and consistent with the predicted Peierls-type BOW/CDW instability in the Dirac-electron layer [12, 13], has been observed in several related Sr/Ca/EuMnSb2 materials [24–34]. Most recently, it was reported in the sister compound YbMnSb2 [35], where the orthorhombic distortion, although much smaller than in other antimonides, is about 50% larger than in CaMnBi2. According to DFT calculations, this distortion leads to a reconstruction of the electronic structure similar to what we find in CaMnBi2 for atomic displacements approximately twice as large as those observed experimentally. Both results are consistent with an electronic Peierls mechanism for the distortion. 

In YbMnSb2, the distortion persists to high 

16 

temperatures, well above magnetic ordering at _T_ N _≈_ 350 K, similar to other antimonides [35]. This led the authors to stipulate that YbMnSb2 crystallizes in an orthorhombic structure whose reduced symmetry governs its electronic properties at all temperatures. In contrast, we find that in CaMnBi2, both crystal and magnetic symmetry are lowered only at low temperature, within the magnetically ordered phase, placing the transition squarely in the regime of low-energy physics governed by electronic interactions. Furthermore, our preliminary measurements [20, 57] indicate that the distortion in YbMnSb2 disappears upon heating to higher temperatures, well below melting, suggesting that it too originates from the same Peierls-type electronic instability of Dirac electrons. 

## **VII. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS** 

Our combined polarized and unpolarized neutron diffraction and x-ray measurements on single-crystal CaMnBi2, supported by DFT, identify the structural and magnetic changes underlying the resistive anomaly at _T ≈_ 50 K [21–23, 47]. We show that _T_ = 46(2) K corresponds to a coupled structural and magnetic symmetry lowering consistent with an electronically driven Peierls-type charge/bond-ordering instability of the Dirac-electron Bi square net [12, 13]. 

Our neutron measurements rule out the previously proposed large ( _≈_ 10 _[◦]_ ) _uniform_ canting of Mn moments in CaMnBi2; any uniform canting, if present, is ≲ 2 _[◦]_ . Therefore, the resistivity/optical anomalies at _T[∗]_ do not originate from spin canting or weak ferromagnetism and the associated Weyl semimetallic state [7, 22, 23, 47]. 

Taken together, our findings establish that the transition at _T[∗]_ in CaMnBi2 represents a rare realization of a two-dimensional Peierls instability toward bond ordering in a Dirac-electron square-net system [12, 13]. The subtle structural distortion, with negligible impact on Mn antiferromagnetism, underscores the primarily Dirac-electron origin of the transition and highlights the broader role of Peierls-type lattice instabilities in shaping the ground states of topological semimetals. Although such transitions have long been studied theoretically [12, 13, 67, 74, 75], experimental realizations have remained scarce or lacking. 

Our results not only bridge this gap, but also carry important implications for materials design. The observed electronic instability of Dirac electrons is sensitive to the Fermi level, which can be tuned compositionally and likely explains the absence of the transition in samples from Ref. 17. The transition temperature _T[∗]_ associated with the resistivity anomaly increases by nearly a factor of two in Ca1 _−x_ Na _x_ MnBi2 with _x_ = 0 _._ 05 [23, 47]. Likewise, systematic variation of the Bi/Sb content in (Ca,Yb)MnBi1 _−x_ Sb _x_ compounds, where Bi-based members show no distortion or only a weak low-temperature Peierls distortion as in CaMnBi2, 

while antimonides exhibit a well-developed distortion, offers a promising route to further experimental and theoretical exploration of electronic instabilities and realizing tunable functionalities in Dirac semimetals.
